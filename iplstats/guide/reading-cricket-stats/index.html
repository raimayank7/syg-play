How to read cricket statistics

Cricket statistics look complicated, but each one answers a simple question. Here are the ones on this site, with the questions they answer and the traps to avoid.

Batting

  • Runs: how many, in total. A big number means a long career or a big season.
  • Average (runs per dismissal): how many runs does the batter score for each time they are out? Not outs are not counted, so a batter who is often not out has a higher average.
  • Strike rate (runs per 100 balls): how quickly do they score? In T20 cricket a strike rate around 130 to 140 is common for the league, but what counts as good depends on the batter’s role and the match.
  • Hundreds and fifties: how often does the batter turn a start into a big score?

Bowling

  • Wickets: how many batters did they dismiss.
  • Economy rate (runs per over): how many runs do they give away? Lower is better.
  • Average (runs per wicket): how many runs are conceded for each wicket? Lower is better.
  • Best figures: the most wickets in an innings, and the fewest runs given in that innings.

Traps

  • Averages and strike rates of players with few innings are unreliable. A batter with three innings and one not out may have a huge average.
  • Eras differ: the T20 league has changed since the first season, so a score that was big in 2008 may be ordinary now. Compare players from the same seasons.
  • Home pitches matter. Our venue pages show how scores differ from ground to ground.

For the sums behind these numbers, such as net run rate and required run rate, you can use any cricket calculator. We explain how our own figures are counted on the methods page.
