Charades formats for teams and big groups

The classic game is for two teams. When you have more players, or want something different, these formats keep the game fresh.

1. Two teams, one actor at a time

The classic format. Teams take turns, each turn has one actor and the whole team guesses. Best for 6 to 14 players.

2. Three rounds, three sets of rules

Use the same 20 words for three rounds. In round one you can speak any word except the one on the card. In round two you can say only one word. In round three there is no speaking at all: pure charades. The words become familiar, so the game gets faster and funnier each time.

3. Relay charades for big groups

Divide the group into teams of five or six and line each team up. The first player of each team sees the word, acts it to the second player, who acts it to the third, and so on, until the last player says what they think the word is. Compare it with the original and enjoy the changes.

4. Family category challenge

Each team writes five words for the other team on paper slips (with a limit on difficulty). It is a good way for families with children and grandparents to be on the same level.

5. Silent party

Play for a short time in a noisy hall with music. People act their word to the person next to them, who has to guess. The room becomes a wave of silent comedy.

Scoring ideas

  • One point for each word guessed.
  • Double points for the hardest word, such as a film title.
  • One bonus point to the actor who gets three words in a row.

Tools for the host

The game page shows words and keeps a timer, so a host can run the game without paper slips. For a teams-and-letters game see also antakshari.
