Charades

Plan a charades party

Charades is one of the easiest parties to plan. You need people, a room and a handful of word lists. Here is a plan for an evening of about an hour and a half.

How many people?

The game works for four players (two teams of two) up to twenty or more (four teams of five). Fewer than four makes the guessing slow, and more than twenty means long waits for a turn, so split the group in two rooms if it is bigger.

A running order

TimeRoundCategory
0 to 10 minWarm-up: everyone plays one turnAnimals or actions
10 to 30 minRound 1, two minutes each teamIndian foods, objects at home
30 to 55 minRound 2, harderPlaces, jobs
55 to 75 minFinaleFilm titles
75 to 90 minWinners and snacks

What you need

  • A phone with the game page open, or paper slips and a bowl.
  • A timer. The game page has one built in.
  • A score sheet. A bit of paper is enough.
  • Seats arranged so that the actor stands in front of the guessers.

Prizes

Small prizes work best: sweets, a pencil box, a certificate for the best actor. Give a funny prize for the funniest turn, as well as for the winning team.

Preparing slips of paper

If you prefer paper to a phone, copy the words of a category from its page and print them, or write them out. Cut the paper into slips, fold each one, and put them in a bowl. Prepare more than you think you need: a lively game uses 30 to 40 words per team. Use a different bowl for each category, so that you can choose the level of difficulty for each round.

Dividing into teams

Mix ages and confidence in each team, so that quiet players are not left out. A simple way is to write the names on slips and draw them out. For a family party, put one grandparent or parent on each team: it is good for the children to see adults making fools of themselves.

Keep it kind

Avoid words that might embarrass a guest. The lists here are clean and family friendly. If you add your own words, keep them kind and suited to the youngest person in the room. For ways to play with big groups see team formats.
