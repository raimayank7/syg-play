How to act in charades: signals and tips

The best charades players are not the best actors, but the best at making themselves clear. These common signals and habits help guessers a lot, and most groups know them already.

Signals many players use

SignalMeaning
Hold up fingers, then tap your armFirst show how many words, then tap the arm for which word you are acting
Touch your ear"Sounds like": the next thing you act rhymes with or sounds like the word
Show a small gap between thumb and fingerA short word, such as "a", "the" or "of"
Wave a hand in a circle, quickly"Keep going, you are close"
Pretend to wind a film cameraThe answer is a film
Open hands like a bookThe answer is a book
Lay fingers across your armThe syllable number, one finger for each syllable

These signals are conventions, not rules. Agree on them before the first round, so that everyone means the same thing.

Tips

  • Start with the category. If the category is animals, the guessers already have a short list in their heads.
  • Make it big. Movements that are bigger than your normal movement are easier to see across a room.
  • Act the easiest part first. For a long phrase, begin with the word you can act most easily and build from there.
  • Do not give up on a word. If your first attempt fails, change the idea instead of repeating it.
  • Use your whole body. Faces help for feelings; hands help for objects; walking helps for places.

For a hard word

Break it into syllables or smaller words. Act one piece, and then the next. For a word like "butterfly", act a stick of butter and a fly. The film titles list is full of words that call for this method.

For the guessers

Shout out anything you think of. Wrong guesses help the actor, because they show what the actor should change. And keep quiet to give a teammate space if they are close.
