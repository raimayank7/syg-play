Can you measure 5 litres with a 4-litre and a 6-litre jug?

This looks like the usual water jug puzzle, but there is a catch: with jugs of 4 and 6 litres it cannot be done. Try it below if you like, then read why.

Why it is impossible

The greatest common divisor of 4 and 6 is 2. Every amount of water that can ever be in either jug is a multiple of 2, because filling, emptying and pouring only ever add or remove whole jugfuls or fill up to a jug's brim. 5 is not a multiple of 2, so it can never appear.

A computer search backs this up: starting from two empty jugs and trying every fill, empty and pour, only 10 different pairs of amounts can be reached, and the amounts that ever appear in a jug are 0, 2, 4, 6. 5 is not among them.

What would make it possible

The rule is simple: a target of at most 6 litres can be measured exactly when the greatest common divisor divides it. With these jugs you can measure 0, 2, 4, 6. To make 5 possible you would need a pair of jugs whose greatest common divisor divides 5, such as a 3-litre and a 5-litre jug.
