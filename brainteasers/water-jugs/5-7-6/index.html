Measure 6 litres with a 5-litre and a 7-litre jug

You have two jugs with no markings, one holding 5 litres and one holding 7 litres, and a tap with as much water as you like. You may fill a jug, empty a jug, or pour from one into the other until the first is empty or the second is full. How can you end with exactly 6 litres in one of the jugs? Try it yourself below, then read the shortest answer.

The shortest solution: 10 moves

A computer search that tries every possible state of the two jugs finds that 10 moves is the least possible. One shortest solution is:

MoveActionJug AJug B
1Fill B07
2Pour B into A52
3Empty A02
4Pour B into A20
5Fill B27
6Pour B into A54
7Empty A04
8Pour B into A40
9Fill B47
10Pour B into A56

Starting by filling the 5-litre jug takes 12 moves, and starting by filling the 7-litre jug takes 10 moves, so the order you start in matters here. The best route takes the shorter of the two.

The solution in words

Move 1: fill b, leaving 0 litres in A and 7 in B. Move 2: pour b into a, leaving 5 litres in A and 2 in B. Move 3: empty a, leaving 0 litres in A and 2 in B. Move 4: pour b into a, leaving 2 litres in A and 0 in B. Move 5: fill b, leaving 2 litres in A and 7 in B. Move 6: pour b into a, leaving 5 litres in A and 4 in B. Move 7: empty a, leaving 0 litres in A and 4 in B. Move 8: pour b into a, leaving 4 litres in A and 0 in B. Move 9: fill b, leaving 4 litres in A and 7 in B. Move 10: pour b into a, leaving 5 litres in A and 6 in B. This is only one of the shortest routes; the search found that no route with fewer moves exists, and you can compare it with your own attempt above. Note how much of the work is pouring back and forth: the trick in most jug puzzles is to create a leftover amount in one jug by filling the other until it overflows in the pouring, then use that leftover as a new starting point.

Why it works

Every amount of water in either jug is a multiple of 1 litre, because every move adds or removes whole jugfuls or pours until one jug is full or empty. The greatest common divisor of 5 and 7 is 1, and the amounts that can ever be measured are exactly the multiples of 1 up to 7. That is why 6 is possible. In this puzzle 24 different pairs of amounts can be reached, and every amount that can appear in a jug is one of 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.
