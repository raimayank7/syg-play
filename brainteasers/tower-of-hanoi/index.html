Tower of Hanoi

Move a stack of discs from one peg to another, one disc at a time, never a big disc on a small one.

The puzzle was published by the French mathematician Édouard Lucas in 1883 and has been a classroom favourite ever since. Pick a number of discs:

  • 2 discs 3 moves
  • 3 discs 7 moves
  • 4 discs 15 moves
  • 5 discs 31 moves
  • 6 discs 63 moves
  • 7 discs 127 moves
  • 8 discs 255 moves

The legend of 64 discs

A story attached to the puzzle says monks move 64 golden discs, and the world ends when they finish. The fewest moves for 64 discs is 18,446,744,073,709,551,615. At one move a second that is about 585 billion years, more than 40 times the age of the universe. The doubling is what makes the number so large: each extra disc doubles the work.

Why the pattern holds

Moving n discs needs one more than twice the moves for n−1 discs, so the counts go 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63 and so on: always one less than a power of two. Our pages show the exact numbers and, for small stacks, every move. Each move list is checked by a program that plays it on a model of three pegs.
