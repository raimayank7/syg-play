Grains on a chessboard: the power of doubling

An old story says an inventor asked a king for one grain of rice on the first square of a chessboard, two on the second, four on the third, and so on, doubling for all 64 squares. The king laughed at such a small prize. How much rice is it?

The total

The total is 264 − 1 = 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 grains. The last square alone holds 9,223,372,036,854,775,808.

A grain of rice weighs roughly 25 milligrams, so the total is roughly 461 billion tonnes, many hundreds of times the yearly world rice harvest (which is well under a billion tonnes). The exact weight of a grain depends on the variety, so treat this as a rough size.

Why the total is one less than the next square

Each square holds one more than all the squares before it put together: 1, then 2 (one more than 1), then 4 (one more than 1 + 2), then 8 (one more than 1 + 2 + 4). So the whole board is double the last square, minus one.
