Tambola for festivals and society events

Tambola is a natural festival game: it needs no equipment beyond tickets and a loud voice, and it works for a crowd of five or five hundred. These ideas help at society functions, temple fairs, office celebrations and family festivals.

Large crowds

  • Print one strip for every six people; tickets from one strip never repeat a number, so a big hall spreads the luck.
  • Use a microphone and, if possible, a board or screen that shows the numbers called. The number caller has a board that can be put on a projector.
  • Have helpers who walk through the crowd to check claims quickly.

Themes

  • Diwali: call numbers with lamp or sweet names, and give diyas, sweets or dry fruits.
  • Lohri and Baisakhi: a bonfire-side game with groundnuts, rewri and gajak as prizes.
  • Holi: use bright coloured tickets, and prizes of herbal colours.
  • Independence Day or Republic Day: small flags and badges as prizes.

Timing

A full game of 90 numbers, with five or six prizes, takes between 30 and 60 minutes depending on how fast the caller goes. For a festival programme, schedule it between two events so it never runs over.

Fairness

At big events, tickets should be handed out in order and should not be duplicated by photocopying: two photocopied strips would have the same numbers. The ticket maker makes a new random strip each time you press the button. See prizes and claims and the hosting plan.

Checklist for a big event

  • Tickets printed and cut at least a day before.
  • A sound system that has been tested.
  • A table with all the prizes and a list of who won what.
  • Helpers with clipboards to take down winners’ names.
  • A rule for a tie, announced before the game.

Safety

For outdoor festivals, keep the game area away from crowds and fires, and make sure children are seated in front. If you give prizes of food, ask about allergies when you hand them out.
