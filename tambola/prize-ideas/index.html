Prize ideas for tambola

A tambola game works best with many small prizes. When four or five players win something, the room stays happy, and nobody has to spend much. Here are ideas arranged by how much they cost, with a guide to matching prizes to patterns.

Nearly free

  • A sweet or a chocolate for early five.
  • A small plant cutting for four corners.
  • A hand-written certificate: "Tambola Champion".
  • The right to choose the next song or the next game.

Small gifts

  • A scented candle or a diya set.
  • A packet of dry fruits or a box of mithai.
  • A notebook and pen, which suits school and office events.
  • A mug, a steel bottle or a lunch box.

Bigger prizes for full house

  • A gift hamper made of local treats.
  • A potted houseplant with a nice pot.
  • A voucher for a local shop or café.
  • A board game for the family.

Matching prizes to patterns

PatternSuggested size of prize
Early fiveSmall: sweet or chocolate
Top, middle, bottom lineSmall gifts, same for all three
Four cornersMedium: something a little special
Full houseLargest prize
Second full houseMedium, so the game stays exciting after the first win

How many prizes to buy

A useful rule is one prize for every four or five players, with at least six prizes in all: early five, three lines, four corners and full house. For a group of 20, that is five or six prizes plus a couple of spares.

For children

Stickers, pencils and small toys work for children. Give every child something, for example a participation sticker, so nobody leaves empty-handed.

See prizes and claims for how the patterns work.

Prizes by occasion

  • Kitty party: beauty items, small kitchen gadgets and tea sets are popular, because prizes there are often used by the winner at once.
  • Family gathering: mix prizes for every age, with a toy or a book for children and something useful for elders.
  • School or class: stationery, a library book, or the privilege of leading the next lesson.
  • Office event: a mug, a desk plant, or a small voucher agreed with the organisers.
  • Neighbourhood festival: local shops often give small items in return for a mention on the event board.

Wrapping and presenting

A plain wrapped prize with a name card makes the win feel special. Put the prizes on a table where everybody can see them from the start: seeing the prizes keeps interest high, especially for children. Announce each prize before it is played for, then hand it over with the winner’s name read aloud and a round of applause.
