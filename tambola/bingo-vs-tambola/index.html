Bingo vs tambola (housie): what is the difference?

People often use the words bingo, housie and tambola as if they were one game, and the games are indeed cousins. The differences are mostly in the cards and the numbers.

Tambola and housie

Numbers go from 1 to 90. A ticket has 3 rows and 9 columns, with 15 numbers and 12 blanks. The prizes are patterns on a ticket: a line, the corners, a full house. This is the style that is played at parties and gatherings across India, and the style our ticket maker prints.

Bingo, the 75-number style

In the common North American version, numbers go from 1 to 75 and a card is a 5 by 5 grid with a free square in the middle. The columns are headed with the letters B, I, N, G, O. The winning patterns are lines across, down and diagonally, and sometimes shapes. Our bingo card maker makes 5 by 5 cards from your own words, which is very popular at baby showers, weddings and office parties.

Which one for your party?

If your guests are mixed in age, or you want many prizes, choose tambola. If you want a game with your own jokes, names or words, bingo cards with your own words are better, because there are no numbers to call: the caller reads out the words or the events as they happen.

Both can use the same prizes. Read prize ideas and the tambola rules.

Other names you may hear

Housie is the name most often used in the British Commonwealth and in India for the 90-number game, and tambola is the name used in much of India. In some places the 90-number game is also called "UK bingo" and the 75-number game "US bingo". Those are labels people use, not official names, and a group is free to call its game whatever it likes.

Can you play one with the other’s tools?

Not easily. A 75-number caller will never call 76 to 90, and a 90-number ticket has no column for the letters B, I, N, G and O. If you want to play 90-number tambola, use the ticket maker and the number caller. If you want the 5 by 5 style, use the bingo card maker, which does not need numbers at all: your words take the place of the numbers.
