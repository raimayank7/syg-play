How to be a good tambola caller

The caller does not win anything, but a good caller makes the game. Here are the habits of callers whom people ask for again and again.

Say the number twice, two ways

Every number is said as a whole and also digit by digit, for instance "fifty-seven, five seven". This avoids the confusions between numbers that sound alike: fifteen and fifty, sixteen and sixty, seventeen and seventy.

Keep a steady pace

About one number every five to eight seconds is comfortable for a mixed room. Faster for experienced players; slower for older relatives. Watch the room, not the clock: if people are still looking at their tickets, wait.

Add a phrase, not a speech

A short phrase such as "forty-eight, a train waiting at the platform" adds humour. It should not be longer than the number. The number names give one for each number, in groups of ten.

Set aside the called numbers

Keep the called numbers in order, either on a sheet or on the number caller board. When someone claims a prize, you read from this record.

Handling a claim

  1. Say "stop" and do not call another number.
  2. Ask the player to read out their numbers.
  3. Tick each against your record.
  4. Announce the result clearly, and thank the player.

If you make a mistake

If you call a wrong number or you are not sure which you called, say so and use the record to correct it. A cheerful correction costs nothing; a disputed prize costs the whole evening.

Look after your voice

Drink water, sit comfortably and hand over to another caller if the game is long.

Calling for different rooms

  • A small family table: a relaxed pace and plenty of chat.
  • A kitty party: brisk, with phrases, and a break for tea after the first full house.
  • A large hall: a microphone, a projector or a large board showing called numbers, and one helper per section to check claims.
  • A video call: type the number as well as saying it, since voices lag on slow connections.

Practice

Before the event, call a few numbers on your own at the pace you will use, and say the confusing pairs aloud: thirteen and thirty, fourteen and forty, fifteen and fifty, up to nineteen and ninety. Saying digits separately removes any doubt.
