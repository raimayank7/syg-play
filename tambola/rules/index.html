How to play tambola (housie): the rules

Tambola, also called housie in many places, is a game of luck for any number of players. A caller draws numbers from 1 to 90 one at a time and announces them. Players hold tickets and cross off the numbers that appear on their own ticket. The first to complete a pattern shouts and wins a prize. Nobody needs any skill, which is why grandparents, children and guests who have never met can all play at the same table.

What you need

  • One ticket for each player (more if they want). A ticket has 3 rows and 9 columns with 15 numbers on it and the rest blank. Our ticket maker prints them.
  • A caller who draws the numbers. You can use a bag of 90 chits or our number caller on a phone.
  • Pens, dried beans or small coins to mark numbers.
  • Prizes agreed before the game starts. See prizes and claims.

How a ticket is laid out

The nine columns hold the numbers 1 to 9, 10 to 19, 20 to 29 and so on up to 80 to 90. Each row has five numbers and four blanks, and every column has at least one number. Within a column the numbers run from small at the top to large at the bottom. A strip is six tickets together that between them contain every number from 1 to 90 exactly once. That is useful when you hand tickets to players, as the page on how many tickets you need explains.

Playing a round

  1. Hand out tickets. Players may write their name on theirs.
  2. The caller draws one number, says it twice (for example "five seven, fifty-seven") and may add a fun phrase. Our number names have one for every number.
  3. Players who have that number cross it out.
  4. Called numbers are put aside, never returned, so the caller can check a claim at the end.
  5. When a player completes a prize pattern they call out "Line!", "Corners!" or "Full house!" (whatever was agreed) at once.
  6. The caller checks the claim against the numbers drawn. A wrong claim usually means that player is out of that prize, but not out of the game.

When to stop a prize

Each prize is won by the first valid claim. If two players claim after the same number, they share the prize or, if it cannot be shared, a quick tie-break is used, such as a coin toss. Agree this before you start, because it is the one moment when friends can disagree.

A short game

A full game to all 90 numbers can be slow with a big room. To speed it up, play for the early prizes only, or call two numbers at once. Children enjoy it more when the pace is brisk.
