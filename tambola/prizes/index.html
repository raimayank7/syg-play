Tambola prizes and how to claim them

The beauty of tambola is that you can invent as many prizes as you like, so many players go home with something. These are the ones most groups use. None is an official rule: every group decides its own list, so say it out loud before the first number is called.

The common prizes

  • Early five: the first player to cross off any five numbers on their ticket. It is a quick prize, good for keeping everyone interested.
  • Top line, middle line, bottom line: the first player to cross off all five numbers of that row of their ticket.
  • Four corners: the first and last numbers of the top row and of the bottom row.
  • Full house: all fifteen numbers on a ticket. This is the big prize and the game normally ends with it.

Other prizes that groups add

  • Second full house and third full house, so the game continues after the first winner.
  • Pyramid or star: a shape on the ticket, agreed by pointing at a drawing before the game.
  • Birthday number: the first to cross off the number that matches the host’s age.
  • Lucky last: the player holding the last number called.

How a claim is checked

  1. The player calls out the prize name.
  2. The caller stops drawing numbers.
  3. The player reads out the numbers of their pattern.
  4. The caller checks each against the set-aside called numbers.
  5. If every number is on the called list, the prize is confirmed. If one is missing, the claim is rejected and the game goes on.

Avoiding arguments

  • Write the prize list on a board or a sheet before the first call.
  • Decide what happens if two people claim after the same number (share, or toss a coin).
  • A claim made after the next number is called is late. Say that early.
  • Keep the called numbers in order, so checking is quick. The number caller shows a board of every called number.

Prizes

Small gifts, sweets, a plant or a voucher work well. We suggest not playing for money: it keeps the game friendly and avoids problems. If prizes are bought with an entry fee, check what the rules of your area or society say about games like this, because they differ. See prize ideas for gifts that do not cost much.
