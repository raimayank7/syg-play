The maths of tambola

Tambola is a game of luck, but the luck follows exact rules, and you can work out what to expect. All the figures below are calculated here from the structure of the game: 90 numbers drawn one by one without replacement, and a ticket of 15 numbers.

Is my number called yet?

After n calls, any particular number has been called with probability n ÷ 90. After 30 calls it is one in three; after 45, half; after 72, four in five.

How many calls does a full house take?

A full house needs all 15 of your numbers to have been called. For a single ticket, the expected number of calls at which that happens is 15 × 91 ÷ 16 = 85.31. Put another way, if only one ticket were playing, the game would typically run to about 85 calls. The game ends sooner in practice because many tickets are playing: someone always finishes earlier.

Each figure is the number of ways to choose the other calls divided by the number of all ways to draw n numbers, so C(n,15) ÷ C(90,15), where C(n,r) means "n choose r".

Why more tickets help only a little

Doubling your tickets gives you roughly twice as many chances at each prize, but also twice as many numbers to watch, and the odds are still set by luck. For the fun of the game, one or two tickets is enough.

Early five

With 15 numbers on your ticket, the chance that at least five of them are among the first n calls grows quickly, and with many players someone usually has it within the first 15 or 20 calls. That is why early five is the quickest prize.

The strip fact

A strip of six tickets contains each number exactly once, so every call lands on exactly one ticket of the strip. If all 90 numbers were called, every ticket of the strip would be full, so a full house is certain to appear somewhere on a complete strip.

Reading the table

The full house table shows how slow a single ticket is: after 70 calls only a small fraction of tickets have a full house, yet in a hall of 100 tickets the first full house comes much earlier, because the best of many tickets is far ahead of the average one. For this reason hosts should not announce "the game ends at call 80". Let the numbers decide.
