Tambola

Bingo card maker

Type at least 24 words, names or phrases, one per line, and get as many different cards as you need. Each card has a free centre square and shuffles your list in a new order.

Ideas for your list

  • Wedding or party: things that always happen, such as "someone dances on the table" or "photographer shouts ‘one more’".
  • Classroom: vocabulary words, chemical symbols, state capitals, numbers.
  • Office meeting: phrases people repeat (kept friendly).
  • Road trip or journey: things to spot from the window, such as a tractor, a temple or a green signboard.
  • Baby shower or birthday: guesses and predictions.

How to play

  1. Give each player a card and a pen.
  2. Players cross out a square when they see or hear that item.
  3. First to cross five in a row (across, down or corner to corner) shouts "Bingo!".
  4. Check the squares and give a small prize.

Notes

The list needs at least 24 different entries, because 24 squares are filled and the middle is free. Use more than 24 to make cards differ from one another. For classic 1 to 90 numbers use the tambola ticket maker. The difference between the games is explained in bingo vs tambola.

Writing a good list

Short entries fit best in the small squares: two to five words. Put likely items and a few unlikely ones together, so that the game has a chance to end but does not end at once. Use 30 to 40 entries for a party of 20, so that cards differ.

Printing

Print on A4 paper. Different players get different cards because each card shuffles the list on its own.
