Noughts and Crosses tips and strategy

Noughts and Crosses is easy to start and rewards practice. These tips come from playing the game many times and from the way it works, with the most useful first. Try one at a time rather than all at once.

Five strategies

Start in a corner or the centre

The best first moves for X are a corner or the centre. If both players play perfectly, the game is a draw; a first move on an edge gives the opponent more chances.

Create a fork

A fork is a move that creates two winning threats at once. Your opponent can block only one, so you win. Look for moves that make two lines of two with an empty third square.

Always block

If the other player has two in a line and the third square is empty, block it at once, unless you can win on this move.

Take the opposite corner

If your opponent starts in a corner, the safest reply as O is usually the centre. A reply on a side square gives them a way to make a fork.

Think about draws

Against the hard computer the best result is a draw. Learning to draw every time is a sign that you have learned the game.

Common mistakes

  • Not blocking the other player’s two in a line.
  • Starting on an edge and missing the strong squares.
  • Playing without looking for a fork.

Going further

Learn the opening theory in three lines. As X, start in a corner or the centre. If X starts in the centre, O must reply in a corner. If X starts in a corner, O must reply in the centre. From there, every move is a block or a threat, and the whole game is a draw if both sides see every threat. To beat the easy computer, aim for a fork: two lines of two that share an empty square, so the computer can stop only one. Try to spot every fork you could make before each move.

A short practice plan

  1. Play three games without thinking about tips, and write down your best.
  2. Read the strategies once. Pick the first one and use only that for the next five games.
  3. Add the second strategy, and so on. Notice which one helps your score most.
  4. After a week, play three games again and compare with your first best. Most players improve clearly.
