How to play Noughts and Crosses

Noughts and crosses, also called tic-tac-toe, is the oldest pencil-and-paper game in many homes. Two players take turns to mark a square in a three-by-three grid, one with X and one with O. The first to make a line of three in a row, column or diagonal wins. Here you play against the computer, which has an easy level and a hard level.

Rules

  1. You and the computer take turns. X always goes first.
  2. Choose whether you play X (first) or O (second).
  3. A line of three of your marks, across, down or diagonally, wins the game.
  4. If all nine squares are filled and nobody has a line, the game is a draw.
  5. Easy mode plays at random. Hard mode looks at every possible future move and never loses.

Controls

Tap or click an empty square. There is nothing to press on the keyboard.

Your first game

Open the Noughts and Crosses game and play one round with no plan, just to see how it feels. Notice what ended the game: that tells you what to watch for next time. Then read the tips and play again, using one tip at a time.

Scoring

A game ends with a win, a loss or a draw, and the page keeps a running record of how many of each you have had on this device. There is no time limit. The hard computer plays perfectly, so against it the best result is a draw; the easy computer plays a random empty square, so you can win by making two threats at once. You can choose whether you play X (first) or O (second), and the computer plays the other.

Scores and saving

Your best score is kept in your own browser, on this device only. If you clear the site data or use a different browser, it will start again from zero. You can send a score to a friend with the Share button at the end of the game.

Questions

Can the hard computer be beaten?

No. If you play perfectly it is a draw, and if you make a mistake the computer takes advantage. We tested it over a thousand games, as first and as second player, and it never lost.

Does easy mode ever win?

Sometimes, by luck, if you are careless. It plays a random empty square each turn.

Is my record saved?

Yes, in your browser: how many games you have won, drawn and lost.
