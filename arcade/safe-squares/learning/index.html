What Safe Squares teaches

Safe Squares is the best logic game on this site. It teaches deduction: working out what must be true from the clues you have. Students learn to be careful, to avoid guesses and to check each step. The rules are simple enough for a child of eight to start, but the hard board can challenge adults.

The main skill: logical thinking

Every game asks something of the player. In Safe Squares it is mostly logical thinking, with a little speed and attention on the side. The skill grows with practice, so a child who plays a few minutes a day for a month tends to play noticeably better, and the thinking habits carry over to schoolwork.

Ideas for parents and teachers

  • Have a class solve a board together on the screen, with students naming the rule each time.
  • Ask children to explain why a square is safe before they open it.
  • Set a challenge: finish the easy board in under two minutes without one guess.

Questions to ask while playing

Talking is where the learning happens. Try these with a child, a student or yourself:

  • Which numbers have all their pits flagged already?
  • Which number has exactly as many closed neighbours as pits?
  • Can I compare these two numbers?
  • Is this really a certain move, or am I hoping?

Which age is it for?

Safe Squares is best from about eight or nine, when children can follow a chain of logical steps. It suits older children, teenagers and adults who enjoy a puzzle with a clear answer. For younger children, play together on the easy board and let them tell you which square they think is safe and why. It is a good way to teach the habit of saying "because" before acting.

A sensible amount

These games are made for short breaks of five to fifteen minutes. A good routine is to play for a few minutes after homework, then do something away from the screen. We show no pop-ups in the game, ask for no purchases and have no chat, so children are not distracted or contacted by others. See our note for parents.
