Join Tiles tips and strategy

Join Tiles is easy to start and rewards practice. These tips come from playing the game many times and from the way it works, with the most useful first. Try one at a time rather than all at once.

Five strategies

Keep your biggest tile in a corner

Choose a corner and never move the big tile out of it. Use only three directions most of the time, and use the fourth direction only when you have no other safe move.

Build a row

Keep your biggest tiles in one edge row in order, with the largest in the corner and the next largest next to it. Rows that follow a staircase of values are easy to join in a chain.

Do not scatter small tiles

Small tiles in the middle of the board block the large ones. Join small tiles as soon as you can, even if it does not increase your score much.

Look before you swipe

Before each move, check what the new tile might do: it can appear in any empty square, including ones that you were hoping to use.

Keep one direction free

Try to keep a row or a column that is always safe to slide in, so that you never get stuck with only one legal move.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the biggest tile out of its corner.
  • Swiping in a direction that scatters the small tiles.
  • Chasing a small score each move and forgetting the shape of the board.

Going further

Advanced players think in rows. Keep your largest tile in a corner, and the next largest next to it along the edge, so that when two equal tiles meet at the end of the row they join in a chain reaction. The most common loss comes from a small tile that gets stuck beside the largest one, so when you see one, make a move that frees it before the board gets crowded. Also practise looking at what the board will look like after the new tile appears in each empty square, not only after your own move.

A short practice plan

  1. Play three games without thinking about tips, and write down your best.
  2. Read the strategies once. Pick the first one and use only that for the next five games.
  3. Add the second strategy, and so on. Notice which one helps your score most.
  4. After a week, play three games again and compare with your first best. Most players improve clearly.
