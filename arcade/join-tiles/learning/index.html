What Join Tiles teaches

Join Tiles is a game of powers of two, a pattern that is useful in maths and computing: 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on. Players also practise planning several moves ahead and thinking about order. It is a good game for teenagers who need to see how doubling grows fast: the 11th tile in a chain is 2048.

The main skill: planning and powers of two

Every game asks something of the player. In Join Tiles it is mostly planning and powers of two, with a little speed and attention on the side. The skill grows with practice, so a child who plays a few minutes a day for a month tends to play noticeably better, and the thinking habits carry over to schoolwork.

Ideas for parents and teachers

  • Ask students to write the tile values as powers of two (2 is 2 to the power of 1) and spot the pattern.
  • Make a table of the first ten tiles with their values, and discuss how quickly it grows.
  • Compare scores with a friend: the same skill, different luck.

Questions to ask while playing

Talking is where the learning happens. Try these with a child, a student or yourself:

  • Which tile is my biggest, and where is it?
  • Which two small tiles can join next move?
  • What happens if the next tile appears in this empty square?
  • Is there a direction I can swipe that changes nothing important?

Which age is it for?

Join Tiles suits older children and adults, from about nine, who are comfortable with doubling. It is a good game for the classroom to show powers of two, since the tiles are 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on. Younger children can enjoy it as a slide puzzle without worrying about strategy. It is a game of patience, and best played in short sessions.

A sensible amount

These games are made for short breaks of five to fifteen minutes. A good routine is to play for a few minutes after homework, then do something away from the screen. We show no pop-ups in the game, ask for no purchases and have no chat, so children are not distracted or contacted by others. See our note for parents.
