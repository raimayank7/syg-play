What Snake teaches

Snake practises planning ahead, spatial thinking and quick, accurate finger movements. Children learn to hold a picture of the board in their head and to think about where something will be, not just where it is now. Teachers use it for a short break; parents like that it has no ads inside the game, no chat and no purchases.

The main skill: planning ahead

Every game asks something of the player. In Snake it is mostly planning ahead, with a little speed and attention on the side. The skill grows with practice, so a child who plays a few minutes a day for a month tends to play noticeably better, and the thinking habits carry over to schoolwork.

Ideas for parents and teachers

  • Ask a child to say aloud where the snake will be in three moves. It trains the habit of thinking ahead.
  • Play in turns, each player taking one apple, so the game becomes cooperative.
  • Set a family best score on a sheet of paper on the fridge.

Questions to ask while playing

Talking is where the learning happens. Try these with a child, a student or yourself:

  • Where will the tail be when the head arrives there?
  • Is there a path out of this corner after I eat the apple?
  • Which side of the board is getting crowded?
  • Could I have waited one move to open a safer route?

Which age is it for?

Snake suits children from about seven, who can hold the board in their head and steer with a finger. Younger children enjoy it with a parent holding the device. Teenagers use it as a challenge to beat their own best, and older players like it because it is calm until the snake grows long. For classroom use, the pause button lets a teacher stop the game for discussion without losing the position.

A sensible amount

These games are made for short breaks of five to fifteen minutes. A good routine is to play for a few minutes after homework, then do something away from the screen. We show no pop-ups in the game, ask for no purchases and have no chat, so children are not distracted or contacted by others. See our note for parents.
