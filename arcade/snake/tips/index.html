Snake tips and strategy

Snake is easy to start and rewards practice. These tips come from playing the game many times and from the way it works, with the most useful first. Try one at a time rather than all at once.

Five strategies

Plan two moves ahead

Look at where the apple is and also where your tail will be in a few moves. Many crashes happen because the snake is boxed in by its own body, not because of the wall.

Make room early

While the snake is short, take the apple by the longest safe route if there is no rush. Keeping the board open on one side gives you space to turn later.

Use the walls

Hugging a wall for part of the journey keeps one side safe. Turning at a wall is safer than turning in the middle of the board when the snake is long.

Do not chase the apple blindly

When the apple is next to your body, go round it instead of squeezing through a gap that will close behind you.

Fill the board in rows

Once the snake is very long, move in a back-and-forth pattern like mowing a lawn, so no gap is left that you cannot get out of.

Common mistakes

  • Turning too late and hitting the wall.
  • Trapping the snake inside its own coil when it is long.
  • Playing too fast with no plan: speed is not the problem, space is.

Going further

For long games, learn the "lawn-mower" pattern: run the snake along rows from one side of the board to the other, turning at the walls, so that every square is used and no gap forms behind you. When the apple appears in the middle of a row you have already cleared, take a short detour and return to the pattern. Many players who reach long lengths use some version of this idea, because it replaces thinking about each move with following a route.

A short practice plan

  1. Play three games without thinking about tips, and write down your best.
  2. Read the strategies once. Pick the first one and use only that for the next five games.
  3. Add the second strategy, and so on. Notice which one helps your score most.
  4. After a week, play three games again and compare with your first best. Most players improve clearly.
