How to play Memory Match

Memory Match is a card game for one player: a board of face-down cards, each with a twin. You turn two over at a time. If they match, they stay face up; if not, they turn back and you try to remember where they were. The fewer moves you take, the better your score.

Rules

  1. Choose a board: 4 by 4 has 8 pairs, 6 by 6 has 18 pairs.
  2. Turn over two cards. If the pictures match, they stay turned up.
  3. If they do not match, they turn back after a short pause. A move is every pair of cards you turn over.
  4. The game is won when every pair is found. Your move count is your score, and fewer is better.
  5. Your best number of moves for each board size is saved in your browser.

Controls

Tap or click a card to turn it over. There is nothing to press on the keyboard.

Your first game

Open the Memory Match game and play one round with no plan, just to see how it feels. Notice what ended the game: that tells you what to watch for next time. Then read the tips and play again, using one tip at a time.

Scoring

Scoring counts moves, not time, so you can take as long as you like. A move is a pair of cards turned over, whether or not they match. The lowest possible number is 8 on the small board and 18 on the large board, which would need perfect luck. A good result on the small board is in the low to middle teens. Your best score for each board size is kept separately in this browser, and the Share button creates a message to send a friend with your number.

Scores and saving

Your best score is kept in your own browser, on this device only. If you clear the site data or use a different browser, it will start again from zero. You can send a score to a friend with the Share button at the end of the game.

Questions

What is the lowest score possible?

On the small board, if you were lucky enough to pick right every time, 8 moves. In practice, 12 to 18 is a good score; on the large board, 30 to 50.

Do the cards change every game?

Yes. The cards are shuffled every time you press New game.

Is it for children?

Yes, but it is a good test for adults on the large board.
