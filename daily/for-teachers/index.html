Daily Puzzles

Daily puzzles for teachers

The puzzles make good five-minute starters: they are fresh every day, free, and need no preparation. Project them on a screen and let the class work in pairs.

By puzzle

  • Number puzzle: practice of the four operations and of brackets and the order of operations. Ask students to explain their sum aloud.
  • Number sequence: pattern spotting, which leads to algebra. Ask for the rule before the answer.
  • Word scramble: spelling and vocabulary. Ask students to use each word in a sentence.
  • Odd one out: visual attention and comparison. A good break after a long lesson.

Routines that work

  1. Show the puzzle at the start of the lesson and give three minutes.
  2. Ask one pair to explain how they solved it.
  3. Keep a class streak on the wall: the number of days in a row that the class solved today’s puzzle.
  4. On Fridays, ask students to make their own puzzle for the class.

Difficulty by weekday

The level follows the weekday: Monday is very easy, rising to hard at the weekend. Teachers can use Monday and Tuesday puzzles for younger children, and the harder days for older students.

Extending the puzzles

  • After the sequence puzzle, ask students to write their own sequence with the same rule and swap with a neighbour.
  • After the number puzzle, ask for a second way to reach the target.
  • After the word scramble, ask students to find three more words that can be made from the same letters.

Time needed

Each puzzle takes between one and five minutes. A whole set, with discussion, fits in a ten-minute starter.
