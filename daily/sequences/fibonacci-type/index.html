Fibonacci-type sequences: Add the two terms before

In a Fibonacci-type sequence each term is the sum of the two before it. The famous one starts 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, but you can start with any two numbers: 2, 5, 7, 12, 19 follows the same rule.

How to spot it

If the gaps themselves look like the earlier terms of the sequence, try adding two terms. A quick test: does the third term equal the first plus the second? If so, check the fourth term against the second plus the third.

Step by step

Take the sequence 2, 1, 3, 4, 7. Step 1: write the gaps between neighbours: -1, 2, 1, 3. Step 2: write the gaps of the gaps: 3, -1, 2. Step 3: write the ratios of neighbours: 0.5, 3.0, 1.33, 1.75. Compare the three lines. The line that is steady (all the same) or follows a simple pattern tells you the rule. Here the rule is: each term is the sum of the two before it (2 + 1 = 3), so the next number is 11.

Common mistakes

Learners expect a fixed gap and are confused when the gaps themselves grow. If the gap between two terms equals an earlier term in the list, think of the add-two-terms rule.

Where you meet this kind of sequence

Sequences in which each term adds the two before appear in counting problems, in the way some plants arrange leaves and petals, and in computer science exercises. They are a favourite of puzzle setters because they look random at first.
