Cube number sequences: The cubes: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125

A cube number is a whole number multiplied by itself twice: 4 × 4 × 4 = 64. Cube sequences grow very fast. The gaps between cubes are not equal, and neither are the gaps of the gaps, but the third differences are all 6.

How to spot it

If the terms grow faster than squares and the numbers end in digits that look like 1, 8, 7, 4, 5, 6, 3, 2, 9, 0, check for cubes. Learn the first ten cubes (1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000) and you will spot them at once.

Step by step

Take the sequence 8, 27, 64, 125, 216. Step 1: write the gaps between neighbours: 19, 37, 61, 91. Step 2: write the gaps of the gaps: 18, 24, 30. Step 3: write the ratios of neighbours: 3.38, 2.37, 1.95, 1.73. Compare the three lines. The line that is steady (all the same) or follows a simple pattern tells you the rule. Here the rule is: these are 2³, 3³, 4³ and so on, so the next number is 343.

Common mistakes

Cubes grow so fast that people think the rule is multiplication. Check the first few terms against the cubes you know: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125. A multiplication by a fixed number would have a constant ratio, but the ratio of cubes keeps falling.

Where you meet this kind of sequence

Cube numbers measure volume: a cube of side 4 holds 64 small cubes. They appear in puzzles about stacking blocks and in simple formulas of physics and chemistry.
