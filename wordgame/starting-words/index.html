The best starting words for Tijori

Your first guess in Tijori is made with no information at all, so it is the one guess you can prepare in advance. A good opener is not the word most likely to be right (no word is likely to be right); it is the word whose result tells you the most, whatever that result turns out to be. We worked this out for every word in the answer list.

How we measured

For each of the 807 answer words taken as a first guess, the program played it against all 807 possible answers using the game's own rule, and recorded the result each time: which dials lock, and how many letters are elsewhere. Answers that give the same result cannot be told apart by that guess, so they form a group. A good opener splits the answers into many small groups. The score in the table is the average size of the group the real answer will be in: the number of words you can expect to remain possible after the first guess. Lower is better.

Why these words win

  • Five different letters. A repeated letter usually wastes a slot. Nearly every word in the table uses five different letters.
  • Common letters, but not only common letters. Because Tijori counts loose letters instead of pointing at them, a guess made of the five most common letters often returns "+2" or "+3", which is hard to read: which two? A slightly less common mix splits the answers more evenly.
  • Letters in their favourite places. A lock is the most useful result, since it names both the letter and the place. Good openers put letters where they most often stand: S, C, B or T first, E or Y last. See letter positions.
  • The zero is welcome. The last column shows how often the opener scores 0. A zero removes five letters for certain, and the keyboard marks them for you.

Openers that are not answers

You may guess any word in the full list, not only the common ones. Among everyday words with five different letters that are not restricted to the answer list, the best scores are: CRANE (65.0), CRATE (65.7), SLATE (66.3), SHARE (67.6), SNARE (67.9), STARE (69.2), TRACE (69.7), ARISE (70.9). The gain over the table above is small, and a guess from the answer list has the extra chance, slight as it is, of being right first time.

A planned second guess

Some players like a fixed pair: two words with ten different letters, played one after the other whatever happens. It is not the best play in every game, since the best second guess depends on the first result, but it is simple and it covers a great deal of the alphabet. How to use the first result

  • 0: all five letters out. Play a second word of five new letters.
  • A lock and nothing else: keep the locked letter in its place and change the other four.
  • "+1" or "+2" with no lock: you know letters are present but not which. Make your next guess share exactly some of the letters, in new positions, so that the change in the count tells you which they were. The strategy guide explains this step by step.

Fair warning

These rankings are averages over the whole answer list. On a given day the answer is one particular word, and the "best" opener may do badly while a poor one gets lucky. Over many games the averages win. The figures were calculated when this site was last built and would change a little if the answer list changed. If you enjoy using the same opener every day, any word from the upper half of the table will serve you well.
