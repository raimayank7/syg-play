How to play Tijori

Tijori is a daily word puzzle made for this site. The name is the Hindi word for a safe or strongbox. A five-letter English word is locked inside, and you have 8 tries to find it. This page gives the rules in full and then plays one game from start to finish.

The rules

  1. Guess a word. Type any five-letter English word and press Enter. It must be a real word from our list; made-up strings of letters are refused and do not use up a try.
  2. Locked letters. Each letter of your guess that is the right letter in the right place locks into its dial at the top. In the list of your guesses, locked letters are shown in a ring.
  3. Letters elsewhere. The game then counts how many of your remaining letters are in the secret word but in a different place, and shows the count: "+1 elsewhere", "+2 elsewhere". It does not tell you which letters these are.
  4. Zero. If nothing locks and the count is zero, none of your five letters is in the word. The keyboard greys them out.
  5. Win or lose. Lock all five dials within 8 tries and the safe opens. If the eighth guess fails, the word is shown.

Repeated letters

A letter is counted only as many times as it occurs in the secret word. If the word is PLANT and you guess TATTY, the count for T is one, not three, because PLANT has one T. If one of the Ts in your guess is in the right place, it locks, and the other Ts then count for nothing. Words with a doubled letter, such as HAPPY or SPEED, can be answers.

An example game

The secret word is PLANT. Here is how a careful player might find it.

  1. 1house0
  2. 2train1 locked+2 elsewhere
  3. 3giant3 locked
  4. 4slant4 locked
  5. 5plantopen
  1. HOUSE scores 0. No H, O, U, S or E in the word. Five common letters are ruled out at once, including three vowels. The word must get its vowels from A, I or Y.
  2. TRAIN locks the A and scores +2 elsewhere. The A is the third letter. Two of T, R, I, N are in the word, in other places than where TRAIN has them.
  3. GIANT locks A, N and T, with nothing elsewhere. The word ends in ANT. Since GIANT has nothing elsewhere, G and I are out. Looking back at TRAIN: its two loose letters must have been T and N (now placed), so R is out as well.
  4. SLANT locks L, A, N, T. The player knew S was out from the first guess, but used SLANT to test the L. It locked: the word is _LANT.
  5. PLANT opens the safe. Few letters fit the first dial. B, C, F, G and P make BLANT, CLANT, FLANT, GLANT and PLANT, and only one of those is a word.

Notice the fourth guess. The player spent a try on a word that could not be the answer, in order to learn about L. In a game that only counts loose letters, a guess chosen to test something is often worth more than a hopeful stab.

The hint

Once in each game you may tap Hint. It opens the first dial that is still shut, showing you one letter in its place. The result you share will say "with a hint". The hint is not offered when only one dial is left.

Marking letters

Tap Mark letters to switch marking on. Now a tap on a key does not type; it changes the key's mark: one tap greys the letter out (not in the word), a second tap rings it (in the word), a third clears it. Tap Mark letters again to go back to typing. Marks are your own notes and can be wrong; the game sets marks itself only when it is certain, after a zero.

The words

You may guess any of {allowed} five-letter words. The answers are drawn from a shorter list of {N} common words, so the answer is always a word you are likely to know. Plurals made by adding S, and past forms made by adding ED, are accepted as guesses but are never the answer. American spellings such as COLOR are accepted as guesses but are not answers.

Daily, archive and practice

The daily puzzle changes at midnight by your device's clock and is the same for everybody. The archive lets you play any earlier date. Practice gives random words without limit. Only the daily puzzle counts towards your results.

There is a Hindi game with three-akshar words: तिजोरी हिंदी में, with its own page of rules, कैसे खेलें.
