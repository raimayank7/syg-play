Tricky words: families, doubles and other traps

Some Tijori puzzles are settled in four guesses and others drag to the eighth with four dials locked and the last one refusing to open. The difference is rarely the player. It is the word. This page collects the kinds of word that cause trouble, counted from the 807 answer words, and says what to do about each.

One-letter families

A family is a set of answer words that are identical except for one letter in the same place: BATCH, CATCH, MATCH, PATCH, WATCH. Lock the other four letters and you are left guessing one dial with many candidates and nothing to tell them apart. The largest families in the answer list:

How to escape a family

Do not guess the family members one by one. With five candidates and three tries left, that is a gamble. Play a test word instead: a word that is not in the family but contains several of the candidate letters. Suppose _ATCH is locked. Five answer words fit: BATCH, CATCH, MATCH, PATCH and WATCH. Guessing them in turn could take five tries. Play BLIMP instead, a word that tests B, M and P at once. If its B locks, the answer is BATCH. If it scores "+1 elsewhere", the answer is MATCH or PATCH. If it scores 0, the answer is CATCH or WATCH. One test has cut five candidates to two at most, and two more guesses are then certain to finish the job.

In Tijori the test word works especially well, because a letter that belongs in the open dial will show up either as a lock, if your test word happens to have it in that position, or as one more letter "elsewhere".

Repeated letters

234 of the 807 answer words (29%) use a letter twice or more. They cause two kinds of trouble. First, players tend not to think of them. Second, the count hides them: if the word is SPEED and you guess THESE, one E locks and the result says "+1 elsewhere" for the other E, which most people read as a different letter. A sample of the answers with repeated letters: adapt, again, agree, ahead, alarm, allow, alpha, amaze, annoy, apart, apple, apply, arena, array, arrow, asset, await, awake, award, aware, banal, bless, bliss, blurb, boost, brass, breed, carry, catch, cease, check, cheek, cheer, chess, chuck, civil, class, click, cliff, clock.

Answers where the two letters stand side by side include: agree, allow, annoy, apple, apply, array, arrow, asset, bless, bliss, boost, brass, breed, carry, cheek, cheer, chess, class, cliff, comma, creed, creep, cross, curry, dress, drill, dummy, error, essay, fizzy.

What to do: when your proved letters number only four and no word fits, try doubling each of them in turn. And when "+1 elsewhere" refuses to be explained by any new letter, ask whether it is a second copy of a letter you have already locked.

Two words, same letters

Anagram pairs among the answers, such as ANGEL and ANGLE or STALE, STEAL and LEAST, can produce a guess with no locks and "+5 elsewhere", or two locks and "+3". You then know every letter and must only find the order. Write the letters in a circle and read round from each one; the methods on our sister site's page on solving anagrams all apply.

Unusual shapes

  • Starting with a vowel: ABOUT, EARLY, OCEAN, UNDER. Players leave the first dial for consonants and forget these.
  • No A, E, I, O or U: a handful of words rely on Y. If every vowel is ruled out, think of Y.
  • Ending in a vowel other than E: PIANO, RADIO, EXTRA, MEDIA, PIZZA.
  • Silent letters: KNIFE, WRITE, GHOST, DOUBT. Sounding out a word in your head will not suggest them; looking at letter patterns will.

The general lesson

When the candidates are few and alike, stop trying to be right and start trying to learn. A guess that cannot be the answer but splits the candidates in half is the strongest move in the game. See the strategy guide for the full method, and try these positions in practice mode.
