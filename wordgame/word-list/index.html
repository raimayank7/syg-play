The words behind Tijori

Two lists of words stand behind the English game: a long one of the words you may type as a guess, and a much shorter one from which the daily answers are taken. This page explains both, and how a word is given to a date.

Where the words come from

Both lists are drawn from SCOWL, "Spell Checker Oriented Word Lists", compiled by Kevin Atkinson and published at wordlist.aspell.net (release of 7 December 2020). SCOWL is an open collection used by spelling checkers. Its licence allows the lists to be used and shared provided its notice is kept, and we publish the notice in full: SCOWL copyright and permission notice. SCOWL sorts words into sizes by how widely they are known, which is what lets us separate everyday words from rare ones.

The guess list: 6,485 words

You may guess any five-letter word from SCOWL's general English lists up to its size 70, which its maker describes as the size of a large dictionary. That includes British and American spellings, plurals, verb forms and a good many unusual words. We removed offensive words. A guess that is not on the list is refused with a message and does not cost a try.

No list is perfect. Now and then a word you are sure of will be refused, because it is a name, a very new word or simply missing; and some accepted words will look strange. If a common word is refused, please tell us.

The answer list: 807 words

An answer should be a word that nearly every player knows, so that losing never feels like a trick. The answers are taken only from SCOWL's two smallest sizes, 10 and 20, which hold the most common words of the language. From those we removed:

  • plurals and verb forms ending in S, and past forms ending in ED;
  • American spellings where British and Indian usage differs (so COLOR and FIBER are not answers, though you may guess them);
  • words that are offensive, or simply too grim to meet over the morning tea;
  • a few abbreviations and oddities that had slipped into the common sizes.

What remains is 807 words: enough for more than two years of daily puzzles without a repeat.

How the daily word is chosen

The answer list was put into a random order once, by program, and that order is fixed. Puzzle No. 1 fell on 1 November 2026; each day takes the next word. When the list is used up, it starts again from the beginning. Nobody picks a word for a date, so there are no themed answers for festivals or news events, and no way for us to know or hint at tomorrow's word short of looking it up in the list.

The date is taken from your own device, so the puzzle changes at your local midnight. People in different time zones play the same numbered puzzle on the same calendar date, at different moments.

Can the answer be found in the page?

The game runs entirely in your browser, so the word list has to be there too. It is lightly scrambled so that nobody meets the answers by accident, but a determined person can dig them out. That is true of any puzzle that works without a server. The only person a cheat can fool is himself.

The Hindi words

The Hindi game uses a list of 256 everyday three-akshar words that we wrote ourselves: things, places, actions and qualities that any Hindi speaker knows, such as किताब, बादल and मिठाई. Words with joined consonants (संयुक्त अक्षर) are left out so that the on-screen keyboard stays simple. Because we have no complete open list of Hindi words to check against, the Hindi game accepts any three akshars as a guess. No native-speaking editor has reviewed the list; if a spelling looks wrong to you, please write to us.

Meanings

The source list has spellings only, so the game cannot show a definition of the day's word. If an answer is new to you, a dictionary will explain it.
