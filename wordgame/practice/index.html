Word Puzzle

Practice: unlimited puzzles

A random word each time, as often as you like. Practice games do not change your daily results.

    What practice is for

    The daily puzzle comes once a day, and one game a day is a slow way to learn. Practice mode gives you a fresh random word whenever you tap New puzzle. The rules are exactly those of the daily game: eight tries, letters in the right place lock in, the rest are counted, one hint. The words are drawn from the same list of common words. Nothing you do here touches your daily results or your streak.

    Experiments worth trying

    • Test a starting word. Play ten games with the same first guess and notice how often it leaves you well placed. Then try another. The page on starting words ranks them by calculation; here you can feel the difference.
    • Learn the one-letter change. After a guess that scores "+2 elsewhere", make a second guess that differs from it by a single letter. If the count drops, the letter you removed was one of the two. If it rises, the new letter belongs. If it stays the same, either both letters are out or both are in. This is the central skill of the game.
    • Play without the hint. The hint opens a dial, which is a great help. Leaving it alone forces you to reason about positions.
    • Play with marks. Tap Mark letters, then tap keys to grey out letters you have ruled out and ring the ones you have proved. Good marking turns a muddle into a short list.
    • Aim for five. Opening the safe in eight tries is comfortable. Opening it in five or fewer, regularly, needs every guess to carry information. Count how many of ten practice games you finish in five.

    A training routine

    1. Warm up with two practice games at your own pace.
    2. Play the daily puzzle with full attention.
    3. If it went badly, replay the same kind of position in practice: for example, if you were caught among several words that differ in one letter, read tricky words and try again.

    For teachers and parents

    Practice mode suits a classroom, because every pupil or pair can have a different word and there is no "one answer of the day" to be whispered across the room. Ask pupils to say aloud what each result proves before they type the next guess. The game rewards a habit that carries into every subject: change one thing at a time and see what happens.
