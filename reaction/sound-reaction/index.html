Reaction Test

Sound reaction test

Turn your sound on. Tap to start, wait in silence, and tap the moment you hear the beep.

Loading...

How the test works

  1. Turn up the volume, or put on earphones.
  2. Tap the box. It says "Wait" and stays the same colour; this time there is nothing to see.
  3. After a random pause, a short beep sounds.
  4. Tap at once. Five tries are averaged for your score.

Tapping before the beep is "Too soon", and that try starts again. If you hear nothing, check that the phone is not on silent; some phones mute web pages when the ringer switch is off.

Sound against sight

It is a long-established finding of experimental psychology that people respond a little faster to a sound than to a light. The usual explanation is that the ear turns a sound into a nerve signal more quickly than the eye does with light, so the message reaches the brain sooner. The difference is small, a few hundredths of a second, but it is steady enough to show up in most people who try both tests several times.

That is why races start with a bang and not with a flag. It is also why sprinters are judged to have made a false start if they leave the blocks sooner after the gun than any human could have reacted: a start that fast must have been a guess.

Why your sound score may not be faster here

On phones and computers, sound output has its own delay, and it is often larger than the screen's. The beep is ordered by this page at a precise moment, but the device may take a few hundredths of a second, sometimes more, to play it. Wireless earphones and speakers add further delay, in some cases a tenth of a second or more. So do not be surprised if your sound score on a phone with wireless earphones is slower than your light score. For the fairest comparison use the device's own speaker or wired earphones.

Compare the two properly

  1. Do the light test three times and note the three averages.
  2. Do this sound test three times on the same device.
  3. Compare the middle value of each set, not the best.

A class or a family can pool results: if most people are quicker to sound, you have repeated a classic experiment at home. If most are slower, you have learnt something about the audio delay of your devices, which is just as interesting.

A test for eyes-free practice

Because nothing changes on screen, you can do this test with your eyes closed. Some people find they concentrate better that way. It also makes the test usable by people who cannot see the colour change in the light test.

This is a game. It is not a hearing test and cannot tell you anything about your hearing or health. If you have concerns about your hearing, see a doctor.
