Afterimage illusion

Stare at the dot for 20 seconds, then look at the white panel.

What to expect

Press the button, then keep your eyes on the black dot for 20 seconds. Do not look around. The panel then turns white. For a moment you should see a pale reddish ghost where the cyan was. It fades in a few seconds, and blinking makes it come and go.

Why it happens

Colour is sensed by three kinds of cone cell in the retina, which respond to long (red), medium (green) and short (blue) wavelengths. Cyan light excites the green and blue cones strongly and the red cones hardly at all. After a long stare the green and blue cones get tired and respond weakly. When white light then arrives, containing all three colours, the red cones respond more than the tired green and blue cones, so the white looks reddish. Red is the opposite, or complementary, colour of cyan.

Try this

  • Close your eyes after the stare: the afterimage sometimes shows against the dark too.
  • Look at a wall farther away and notice that the afterimage grows. It stays the same size on your retina, but looks bigger on a more distant surface.
  • Try again after a minute with a different starting colour using paint or printed sheets.

A safety note

Do not use a bright light such as the sun or a torch for afterimages; it can hurt your eyes. A phone or computer screen at normal brightness is fine, and you can stop at any time.
