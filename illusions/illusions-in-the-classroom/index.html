Optical illusions in the classroom

Illusions are a rare thing in teaching: they surprise everyone, they cost nothing and they teach a lesson that lasts, that seeing is not the same as measuring. This page gives a plan for a thirty minute lesson and some follow-up ideas for ages 7 and up.

A thirty minute plan

  1. Predict (5 minutes). Show the Müller-Lyer picture without the red lines. Ask the class to vote on which line is longer.
  2. Measure (5 minutes). Let pairs measure the lines on a printout with a ruler. Then use the button to show the guides.
  3. Discuss (5 minutes). Ask: why did we all agree and all get it wrong? What does that tell us about trusting our eyes?
  4. Explore (10 minutes). Each group takes one illusion, predicts, then tests.
  5. Draw (5 minutes). Ask each group to draw one illusion on paper with a ruler.

Good questions

  • Did it help to know the trick before looking again?
  • Do you think everyone sees it the same way?
  • Why might the brain take shortcuts?
  • Where might this go wrong in real life: a road, a chart, a map?

Extension ideas

Older pupils can count how many people in a sample are fooled and draw a bar chart, or measure how long the lines look in centimetres by drawing a comparison line. The afterimage works well as a quick science demonstration. Use a normal screen brightness, never a bright lamp.
