How to play Kakuro

Kakuro is a number puzzle in the shape of a crossword. The grid has dark squares with clues and white squares for digits. Each clue is a total for the run of white squares next to or below it. The aim is to fill every white square with a digit from 1 to 9 so that every run adds up to its clue and no digit repeats inside a run.

Reading the clues

A dark square may show two numbers separated by a diagonal line. The number at the top right is the total for the squares to its right. The number at the bottom left is the total for the squares below. A run ends at a dark square or the edge of the grid. Every run has at least two white squares, and every white square belongs to one run across and one run down.

First steps

  1. Look for the unique sums. A total of 3 in two squares must be 1 and 2; 4 in two squares must be 1 and 3. The cheat sheet lists them. You will not know the order yet, but pencil both digits in.
  2. Use the crossing run. If a square can be 1 or 2 across but the run down already contains a 1, it must be 2.
  3. Think about the biggest and smallest digits. A total of 17 in two squares can only be 8+9.
  4. Count what is left. If four squares must add to 30, the digits are 6, 7, 8 and 9, and none of them can be 1 to 5.

Habits of good solvers

Write the possible digits in each square in pencil. Work on the runs with the fewest possibilities first. When a square is fixed, remove that digit from the other squares in both of its runs. If you get stuck, look for a run where two squares must share the same two digits: those digits can then be removed from every other square in the run.

Starter digits

Our puzzles sometimes show a few grey starter digits. They are there so that the puzzle has only one solution. You cannot change them. Play a small puzzle to practise.
