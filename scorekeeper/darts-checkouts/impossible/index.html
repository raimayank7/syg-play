Scores you cannot finish in three darts

The highest finish in darts is 170: two treble 20s and the bull. Between 2 and 170 there are seven scores that no three darts can take to zero while ending on a double: 159, 162, 163, 165, 166, 168, 169. We found this by trying every combination of three darts and checking each one against the rule that the last dart must be a double.

Why those seven

Each of them needs the first two darts to make a total that no pair of darts can make, once the last dart is chosen as a double. For example:

  • 159: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 109 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 109 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 109 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).
  • 162: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 112 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 112 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 112 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).
  • 163: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 113 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 113 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 113 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).
  • 165: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 115 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 115 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 115 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).
  • 166: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 116 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 116 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 116 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).
  • 168: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 118 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 118 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 118 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).
  • 169: the biggest possible last dart is the bull (50), which would need 119 from the first two darts, and two darts cannot make 119 (the highest score with a dart is 60, and 119 cannot be written as a sum of two dart scores).

The pattern is that scores just under 170 need two high treble darts, and the numbers that are one or two short of a clean treble sum leave a remainder no double can fill. Players with such a score on the board usually take a set-up shot to leave a finishable score for the next visit.

What to do instead

With 159 left, many players throw treble 20, treble 19 and then aim for 42, a finishable score for the next visit. See the chart for 111 to 170 to see what scores below are open.
