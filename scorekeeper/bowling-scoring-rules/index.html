Bowling scoring rules

Bowling scoring looks hard because a frame's score depends on rolls that come later. Once you see the rule, it is simple. This page explains it with examples that were worked out by a program.

The rules

  • Open frame: not all ten pins down in two rolls. Score = pins knocked down.
  • Spare: all ten down with two rolls. Score = 10 + the next one roll.
  • Strike: all ten with the first roll. Score = 10 + the next two rolls.
  • Tenth frame: a strike earns two bonus rolls and a spare one bonus roll, so the tenth frame can have three rolls. Bonus rolls are counted only inside the tenth frame.

Why 300 is the top score

A perfect game is twelve strikes in a row: ten frames plus two bonus rolls in the tenth. Each of the first eight frames scores 30 (10 + 10 + 10), the ninth scores 30 and the tenth 30, giving 10 × 30 = 300. No game can score more, because each frame is worth at most 30.
