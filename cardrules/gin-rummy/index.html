Gin Rummy: rules and how to play

Gin Rummy is a rummy card game for 2 players, played with one standard 52-card deck. It suits ages 10 and up and takes about 20 to 40 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

Make "melds" (sets and runs) from your cards and keep the points of unmatched cards, called deadwood, as low as possible. Reach 100 points before your opponent.

Set-up and the deal

Deal 10 cards to each player. Place the next card face up to begin the discard pile, and the rest face down as the stock. Ace is low in runs and counts 1 point; face cards count 10; the others count their number. A set is three or four cards of the same rank; a run is three or more consecutive cards of the same suit.

By the numbers: After 10 cards go to each player and one is turned up, the stock holds 31 cards. There are 15,820,024,220 different 10-card hands.

How to play

  1. The non-dealer may take the face-up card or pass; if they pass, the dealer may take it; if both pass the non-dealer draws from the stock.
  2. On each turn a player draws the top card of the stock or the discard pile, then discards one card, so their hand stays at 10 cards.
  3. A player may "knock" and end the hand when the points of their unmatched cards are 10 or fewer, by discarding and putting their cards face down.
  4. The opponent then shows their cards, and can lay off cards onto the knocker's melds to reduce their own deadwood.
  5. If a player melds all 10 cards, it is "gin" and the opponent cannot lay off.

Scoring and winning

If the knocker has less deadwood than the opponent, the knocker scores the difference. If the opponent has equal or less deadwood, it is an "undercut" and the opponent scores the difference plus a bonus (commonly 25). Gin scores a bonus (commonly 25) plus the opponent's deadwood. A game bonus of 100 and a bonus for each hand won are also common. House rules differ, so agree them before you start.

Common variations

  • In Oklahoma Gin, the first upcard decides the knock limit.
  • In Straight Gin, there is no knocking: play continues until gin.
  • Hollywood Gin keeps three scoring columns to play several games at the same time.

Tips

  • Discard high unmatched cards early: they cost the most deadwood.
  • Watch what your opponent picks up from the discard pile: it shows which cards they need.
  • Do not knock too early if you can wait for gin; but do not wait too long when your opponent is close.

Questions

What counts as deadwood?

Cards that are not in a set or run. They count against you as points.

When can I knock?

When your deadwood is 10 points or fewer.
