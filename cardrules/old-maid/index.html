Old Maid: rules and how to play

Old Maid is a matching card game for 2 to 8 players, played with one 52-card deck with one queen removed (51 cards). It suits ages 5 and up and takes about 10 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

PlayersCardsAgeTimeType
2 to 8One 52-card deck with one queen removed (51 cards)5 and up10 minutesMatching

Aim of the game

Avoid being the player left holding the odd queen, the "Old Maid", when all other cards have been paired and discarded.

Set-up and the deal

Remove one queen from the deck (often the queen of clubs). Deal all the cards one at a time, face down, so some players may have one card more than others. Look at your hand, and put down any pairs of the same rank (two kings, two fours, and so on). Keep your hand hidden.

By the numbers: With 51 cards the deal is 17 each for three players and 13, 13, 13, 12 for four, so with four players some start with one card more than the others.

How to play

  1. The player to the dealer's left offers their cards, fanned face down, to the player on their left.
  2. That player takes one card without seeing it. If it makes a pair with a card in their hand, they put the pair down.
  3. They then offer their hand to the next player, and so on around the table.
  4. Players who run out of cards are out of the game. Play continues until only one card is left.

Scoring and winning

The player holding the last card, which is always the queen without a partner, is the Old Maid and loses. There is no other score.

Common variations

  • In a shortened game, deal only part of the deck.
  • Instead of removing a queen, some play with a special Old Maid card.

Tips

  • Do not look at where the queen is in your hand when someone offers you the choice, because it shows. Keep your face still.
  • Move the queen around in your hand before you offer your cards so the person choosing cannot tell by position.
  • Take cards from the middle or the ends at random so that the other player cannot predict you.

Questions

Why remove a queen?

With one queen removed the other three queens cannot all be paired, so one unpaired queen remains. The pairing leaves exactly one card with no partner.

Can three of a kind be put down?

Only pairs go down. With three of a kind you put down two and keep one.
