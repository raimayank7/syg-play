Klondike Solitaire: rules and how to play

Klondike Solitaire is a solitaire card game for 1 players, played with one standard 52-card deck. It suits ages 7 and up and takes about 5 to 15 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

Move all the cards to four foundation piles, one per suit, building each from ace up to king.

Set-up and the deal

Deal seven piles in a row, left to right: the first has 1 card, the second 2, and so on up to 7. Only the top card of each pile is face up. The remaining 24 cards form the stock, face down.

By the numbers: The seven piles hold 28 cards in all (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7), leaving 24 in the stock.

How to play

  1. Build down on the seven piles in descending order and alternating colours: a red six can go on a black seven.
  2. Move one card or a correctly ordered group between piles. When you uncover a face-down card, turn it over.
  3. Only a king (or a group starting with a king) can be moved to an empty pile.
  4. Turn over one card at a time (or three) from the stock to the waste pile; the top card of the waste can be played to the piles or the foundations.
  5. Build the foundations up in suit from ace to king. A card can go to a foundation from the waste or the end of a pile.

Scoring and winning

You win when all 52 cards are in the foundations. In Vegas or standard scoring, points are given for each card moved to a foundation. Not every deal can be won, even with the best play.

Common variations

  • Turn one card at a time for an easy game, or three for a harder one.
  • In "Vegas" style, the game is played with limited passes through the stock.
  • Some versions let you move cards from the foundation back to the piles.

Tips

  • Turn over the face-down cards in the biggest piles first.
  • Do not play an ace or two to the foundation too quickly if you may need them later as a place for a card of the other colour.
  • Think before you empty a pile unless you have a king to put in it.

Questions

Can every game be won?

No. Some deals cannot be won however well you play, and many can be won only with careful play.

What is the difference between turning one and three cards?

Three-card turn gives fewer cards that can be reached, so it is harder.
