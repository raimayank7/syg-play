Indian Rummy (13 Cards): rules and how to play

Indian Rummy (13 Cards) is a rummy card game for 2 to 6 players, played with two decks for three or more players (one deck for two) with printed jokers. It suits ages 12 and up and takes about 20 to 40 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

Arrange your 13 cards into valid groups, with at least two sequences of which one is "pure", and "declare" before your opponents.

Set-up and the deal

Each player is dealt 13 cards. One card is turned up to start the discard pile and the rest form the closed stock. A card is chosen at random as the "wild joker", and its rank acts as a joker; printed jokers also act as jokers. Aces can be high or low in sequences, but not both in the same sequence.

By the numbers: With one deck there are 635,013,559,600 different 13-card hands. With two decks the total number of cards is 104, plus any jokers.

How to play

  1. A sequence is three or more consecutive cards of the same suit. A pure sequence has no joker. A set is three or four cards of the same rank in different suits.
  2. On your turn, draw one card from the stock or the discard pile, then discard one card. Your hand always stays at 13.
  3. A valid declaration needs at least two sequences, one of which is pure. The rest can be sequences or sets, with jokers as substitutes.
  4. You declare by discarding your last card face down in a "show" area, and the others then show their cards.

Scoring and winning

The declarer scores 0. Each other player scores the points of their ungrouped cards (ace, king, queen and jack are 10, others face value), up to a maximum of 80. Jokers count 0 in valid groups. A wrong declaration usually scores the maximum, 80. In pool rummy the players with 101 or 201 or more points are out. Rules vary among groups and online platforms, so check them.

Common variations

  • Points rummy: each point has a value in money in some groups, but never play for money if it is against the law where you live.
  • Pool rummy: players are out above 101 or 201 points.
  • Deals rummy: a fixed number of deals is played and the lowest total wins.

Tips

  • Make a pure sequence first: without one you cannot declare.
  • Discard high unmatched cards early.
  • Keep jokers to help complete sets and impure sequences, not as spares.

Questions

What is a pure sequence?

A sequence made without any joker, with consecutive cards of one suit.

Do the rules vary?

Yes. Points for drops, the maximum for a wrong show and the number of decks differ by group and platform.
