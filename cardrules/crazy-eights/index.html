Crazy Eights: rules and how to play

Crazy Eights is a shedding card game for 2 to 7 players, played with one standard 52-card deck (two decks for more than 5 players). It suits ages 6 and up and takes about 10 to 20 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

Be the first to get rid of all your cards by playing a card that matches the suit or rank of the top card on the discard pile.

Set-up and the deal

Deal 7 cards to each player with two players, and 5 each with three or more. Put the rest face down as the stock and turn the top card face up beside it to start the discard pile. If that card is an eight, bury it in the stock and turn another.

How to play

  1. The player to the dealer's left goes first. On your turn, play one card from your hand that matches the suit or the rank of the top card of the discard pile.
  2. An eight is wild: you can play it at any time, and you then say which suit must be played next.
  3. If you cannot play, draw cards from the stock until you can play one, or, in some house rules, draw only one card and pass if it does not fit.
  4. If the stock is used up, shuffle the discard pile (except its top card) to make a new stock, or pass.
  5. The first player to play their last card wins the round.

Scoring and winning

In a round-by-round game the winner scores the value of the cards left in the other players' hands: 50 for each eight, 10 for a king, queen or jack, 1 for an ace and the face value for the rest. The first to reach 100 (or another agreed total) wins.

Common variations

  • A common variation makes a 2 force the next player to draw two cards, a queen skip, or an ace reverse direction.
  • Some groups play that you cannot finish with an eight.
  • Commercial games have been made from the same idea, so the rules of "Crazy Eights" are in many family games.

Tips

  • Save eights for when you are stuck or for your last card, because they are your most valuable cards.
  • Change the suit to one that you have a lot of and others have shown they lack.
  • Notice which suits other players pass on: they probably have none of it.

Questions

Can I finish with an eight?

Yes in the usual rules, but many groups ban it so the last card cannot be a wild one. Agree before starting.

How many cards for two players?

Seven each is the usual deal for two, and five each for three or more.
