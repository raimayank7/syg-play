Whist: rules and how to play

Whist is a trick-taking card game for 4 in two partnerships players, played with one standard 52-card deck. It suits ages 10 and up and takes about 30 to 60 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

With your partner, win more tricks than the other pair over the hands of the game and reach the agreed score first.

Set-up and the deal

Four players, partners opposite. Cut for the deal; the dealer deals all 52 cards, 13 each, one at a time. The last card dealt is turned face up and its suit becomes trumps for the hand, and the dealer takes it into their hand when they first play.

By the numbers: The chance that your 13-card hand holds seven or more cards of a single suit is 4.0%, which is why long suits are worth watching.

How to play

  1. The player to the dealer's left leads any card.
  2. Each other player, in turn, must follow suit if they can. If they cannot, they may play a trump or any other card.
  3. The highest trump wins the trick if any is played, and otherwise the highest card of the suit led wins.
  4. The winner of the trick collects the four cards, turns them face down as a "trick" and leads the next card.
  5. Play continues until all 13 tricks have been played.

Scoring and winning

A partnership scores one point for each trick won beyond the first six (the "book"). The usual game is won by the first partnership to reach 5 points, though some play to 7 or more. Players keep the tricks in front of them as a scoreboard.

Common variations

  • In Bid Whist, the players bid for the number of tricks and the trump suit.
  • In Minnesota Whist, each player tries to win seven tricks.
  • In Knock-out Whist, players drop out when they win no tricks.

Tips

  • Return your partner's lead when you can, because it shows trust in their suit.
  • If you hold the highest card of a suit, play it first when you can.
  • Count trumps as the hand goes on so you know how many remain.

Questions

Is Whist the same as Bridge?

Whist is the ancestor of bridge. It has no bidding, which makes it a simpler game.

What is the book?

The first six tricks won by a partnership do not count: points start from the seventh trick.
