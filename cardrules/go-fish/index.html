Go Fish: rules and how to play

Go Fish is a matching card game for 2 to 6 players, played with one standard 52-card deck. It suits ages 5 and up and takes about 10 to 15 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

2 to 6One standard 52-card deck5 and up10 to 15 minutesMatching

Aim of the game

Collect the most "books", a book being all four cards of one rank (four kings, four sevens and so on).

Set-up and the deal

Shuffle and deal 7 cards to each player when two or three play, and 5 cards each with four or more. Put the rest face down in the middle as the "pond". Look at your hand and keep it hidden. If you are dealt a book at the start, lay it down.

By the numbers: In a 7-card deal, the chance of being dealt at least one pair of the same rank is 79.0% (worked out by counting hands), so most hands begin with something to ask for.

How to play

  1. The player to the dealer's left goes first. On your turn, ask any one player for a rank you already hold in your own hand, for example "Do you have any sevens?"
  2. If that player has cards of that rank, they must give you all of them, and you ask again.
  3. If they have none, they say "Go fish". You draw the top card of the pond. If it is the rank you asked for, show it and take another turn. Otherwise your turn ends.
  4. Whenever you collect all four of a rank, lay the book face up in front of you.
  5. If you run out of cards, you draw from the pond (or, in some groups, take up to five cards) so that you can continue.

Scoring and winning

The game ends when all 13 books are down. The player with the most books wins. If two players tie, share the win or play again.

Common variations

  • Some groups let you ask only for a rank you hold; others do not enforce this. We recommend enforcing it, because it is what makes the game a memory game.
  • For small children, play with only a few ranks, or with picture cards, so there are fewer cards to remember.
  • In a simple version, books are pairs and the player with the most pairs wins.

Tips

  • Remember what each player has asked for: if a player asked for sevens, they hold at least one seven. Ask them later.
  • Ask the player who is most likely to have the card, not the player who happens to sit next to you.
  • Keep your hand tidy by rank so you can see what you can ask for.

Questions

Can I ask for a rank I do not hold?

In standard rules, no: you must hold at least one card of the rank you ask for.

What if the pond runs out?

Play continues without drawing; players who cannot ask simply pass until all books are down.
