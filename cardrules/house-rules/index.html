House rules: agree before you play

Almost every card game has more than one version. The arguments at the table are not about who is right, but about who is playing which version. The simplest fix is to agree the rules before the first card is dealt. This short checklist works for any game on this site.

Before you deal

  1. Number of cards. How many cards does each player get? (Go Fish and Crazy Eights change the deal with the number of players.)
  2. Scoring. What does each card or trick score, and what total ends the game? Write it on paper.
  3. Special cards. Do eights, twos or queens do anything special in your game?
  4. Mistakes. What is the penalty for playing out of turn or showing a card by accident?
  5. Ending. How does the game end: a set score, a number of rounds or a time limit?

When rules clash

If two players know different versions, play the simplest version or the one the youngest player knows. If you play with a group often, write the house rules on a card and keep it with the deck.

Money

We describe card games as games for fun. If you play for money or prizes, check the laws where you live: gambling is regulated, and what is a friendly game in one place can be illegal in another.
