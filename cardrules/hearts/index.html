Hearts: rules and how to play

Hearts is a trick-taking card game for 4 (3 to 5 variants exist) players, played with one standard 52-card deck. It suits ages 9 and up and takes about 30 to 60 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

Finish the game with the lowest score. You score points for taking hearts and the queen of spades in tricks, so you try to avoid taking them.

Set-up and the deal

Each of four players gets 13 cards. Before the first trick, each player passes three cards face down to another player: to the left in the first hand, to the right in the second, across in the third and no passing in the fourth, then the cycle repeats.

By the numbers: There are 635,013,559,600 different 13-card hands, and the chance of being dealt no hearts at all is 1.28%. The 26 points in each hand are 13 hearts at 1 point plus the queen of spades at 13.

How to play

  1. The player with the two of clubs leads it to start the first trick.
  2. Each player must follow suit if they can. A player who has no cards in the suit led may play any card, a "discard".
  3. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, and that player takes all the cards in it and leads the next trick.
  4. You cannot lead a heart until a heart has been played (hearts are "broken"), unless you have only hearts.
  5. Most groups do not allow hearts or the queen of spades to be played on the first trick.

Scoring and winning

Each heart you take scores 1 point and the queen of spades scores 13, so there are 26 points in each hand. The game ends when someone reaches 100 points, and the player with the lowest score wins. If a player takes all 13 hearts and the queen of spades ("shooting the moon"), that player scores 0 and each other player scores 26, or in some groups the moon-shooter takes 26 off their own score.

Common variations

  • Some groups score the jack of diamonds as minus 10.
  • With three players, remove the two of diamonds and deal 17 cards each.
  • With five, remove the two of diamonds and the two of clubs and deal 10 each.

Tips

  • Pass high cards, particularly the queen of spades and other spades above it, unless you hold many low spades to protect the queen.
  • Try to get rid of cards in one suit early so you can discard high cards in it when you have none.
  • Keep track of whether anyone is taking all the hearts: if one player is, break up their run with a late heart.

Questions

Why is the queen of spades so important?

It is worth 13 of the 26 points in a hand, so it is the card everyone tries to avoid taking.

What does shooting the moon mean?

It means taking all the point cards in a hand. It is risky but turns the scoring upside down.
