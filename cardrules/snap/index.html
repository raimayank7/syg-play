Snap: rules and how to play

Snap is a reaction card game for 2 to 6 players, played with one standard 52-card deck. It suits ages 4 and up and takes about 5 to 10 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

Win all the cards by being the first to shout "Snap!" when two cards of the same rank are turned up one after the other.

Set-up and the deal

Shuffle and deal all the cards face down, one at a time, so each player has a pile. Players must not look at their cards. Sit so that all players can reach the middle.

How to play

  1. Players take turns, going clockwise, to turn over the top card of their own pile and place it face up in the middle, in a pile of its own for each player.
  2. Always turn the card away from you so that everybody can see it at the same time as you.
  3. When the top cards of two players' face-up piles show the same rank (two nines, two jacks), the first player to shout "Snap!" wins both piles and adds them to the bottom of their own pile.
  4. If two players call at the same time, the pile is shared or the pile stays and play goes on.
  5. A player who calls "Snap!" by mistake must give a card to each other player, or lose a turn, depending on house rules.

Scoring and winning

A player is out when they have no cards left. The last player with cards wins. In a short game, the player with the most cards after a set time wins.

Common variations

  • For young children, use picture cards that match by animal.
  • Match by suit or colour for an easier version.
  • In "Animal Snap", players call the animal sound of the picture instead of Snap.

Tips

  • Look at the pile of the player who has just turned a card, not at your own, because the match is made by their card.
  • Keep your hand ready over the pile without covering it.
  • Calm hands win: the fastest call is no use if it is wrong.

Questions

What if two players call together?

Decide beforehand: either the pile is split or the call is repeated. The usual rule is that the player whose hand reaches the pile first wins.

Can I play with two players?

Yes, it is a good two-player game and moves fast.
