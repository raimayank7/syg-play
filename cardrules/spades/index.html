Spades: rules and how to play

Spades is a trick-taking card game for 4 in two partnerships players, played with one standard 52-card deck (jokers removed). It suits ages 10 and up and takes about 45 to 90 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

Aim of the game

With your partner, win at least the number of tricks you bid together, and reach 500 points (or another agreed total) before the other pair.

Set-up and the deal

Four players sit in two partnerships, partners opposite. Deal all 52 cards, 13 to each player. Spades are always the trump suit.

How to play

  1. Starting left of the dealer, each player bids the number of tricks they expect to win. The two bids of a partnership are added together. A bid of "nil" (zero tricks) is allowed, for a bonus.
  2. The player left of the dealer leads the first card. Players must follow suit if they can, and otherwise may play any card, including a spade (trump).
  3. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, unless a spade is played, in which case the highest spade wins.
  4. You cannot lead a spade until a spade has been played ("spades are broken"), unless you have only spades.
  5. The winner of a trick leads the next.

Scoring and winning

A partnership that wins at least its bid scores 10 points for each trick bid and 1 point for each extra trick (an "overtrick", or "bag"). If it wins fewer, it loses 10 points for each trick bid. A common rule gives a penalty of 100 points when a partnership collects ten bags. A successful nil bid scores 100 points and a failed one loses 100. These scores vary between groups, so agree them before you start.

Common variations

  • Some groups play with the two jokers and the deuces of clubs and diamonds as high trumps.
  • In variants such as Mirror spades, the bidding rules are changed to force particular bids.
  • Blind nil: a bid of nil made before looking at the cards, for 200 points, is played in some groups.

Tips

  • Count your sure tricks: high spades, aces and kings, and add them when you bid.
  • Bid a little low so that you avoid penalties, but not so low that you accumulate bags.
  • Watch your partner's plays and lead the suit they have shown strength in.

Questions

What is a bag?

A bag is an extra trick over your bid. Ten of them cost you points in the usual scoring, so careful bidding matters.

Is it the same as bridge?

No. It is related to bridge and whist, but the bidding and trump are simpler.
