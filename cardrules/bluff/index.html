Bluff (Cheat): rules and how to play

Bluff (Cheat) is a bluffing card game for 3 to 10 players, played with one 52-card deck (two decks for more than 6 players). It suits ages 8 and up and takes about 15 to 30 minutes. The rules below are the ones most groups use; where groups differ, the page says so.

3 to 10One 52-card deck (two decks for more than 6 players)8 and up15 to 30 minutesBluffing

Aim of the game

Be the first player to get rid of all your cards by playing them face down, telling the truth or lying about what they are.

Set-up and the deal

Deal all the cards, face down, one at a time. Players keep their cards hidden. The game starts with aces.

How to play

  1. The first player puts one or more cards face down in the middle and says how many there are and what rank they are, saying "one ace" or "two aces".
  2. The next player must play cards and call the next rank in order (twos, then threes and so on up to kings and then back to aces).
  3. The cards played may be true or not, but the player must name the correct rank.
  4. Any other player may say "Bluff!" (or "Cheat!") after a play. The cards are turned over. If the player lied, they take the whole pile into their hand. If they told the truth, the challenger takes the pile.
  5. The player who takes the pile starts the next round with the next rank in turn.

Scoring and winning

The first player to have no cards left wins. A player is only winning once their last cards have survived a challenge, so keep watching.

Common variations

  • Some groups allow only one card at a time, or only a fixed number of cards.
  • In "I Doubt It", the same rules are played with different names.
  • Bluffs may be called at any time before the next player plays.

Tips

  • Lie about a small number of cards, because larger plays are challenged more.
  • Keep a straight face and an even speaking tone: speed and emphasis give you away.
  • Remember which ranks you have played: if the other players hold all the aces, a call is likely to be true.

Questions

Can I say how many cards I played?

Yes, you say how many cards and which rank. The number must match the number of cards you put down.

What if the pile is already large?

A large pile is a big penalty to the loser of the challenge, so players challenge more carefully.
