Nonogram rules

Nonograms are puzzles of logic in which you reveal a hidden picture. They were invented in Japan in the late 1980s and are known by many names: picross, griddlers, hanjie and paint by numbers. The rules are short and never change.

Reading the clues

The numbers to the left of a row, and above a column, describe the filled squares in that line from left to right or top to bottom. "4" means four filled squares in a row. "2 3" means a run of two filled squares and, somewhere later in the line, a run of three, with at least one empty square between them. The order of the runs always matches the order of the numbers. A line with only a zero is empty.

How a puzzle is complete

The puzzle is solved when every row and every column matches its clue exactly. You do not have to cross out the empty squares for the puzzle to count, though crosses help you to think. On this site a banner tells you when every clue is satisfied.

One answer

Good puzzles have exactly one solution, and good solvers never guess. All puzzles here are tested with a logic-only solver; if it cannot finish the puzzle without guessing, the puzzle is thrown away and another is made. Read how to solve for the techniques.

Sizes

A 5 by 5 puzzle takes a minute or two, a 10 by 10 takes ten to twenty minutes for a new player, and 15 by 15 puzzles take longer. Start small and move up when the small ones feel easy.
