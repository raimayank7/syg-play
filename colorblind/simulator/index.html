Colour blindness simulator

Pick two colours and see whether they stay different for people with colour vision differences.

How to use it

Click each colour box and choose a colour. The tool shows the pair as seen with normal vision and as simulated for protanopia (no working red cones), deuteranopia (no working green cones), tritanopia (no working blue cones) and in greyscale. Below each pair is the contrast ratio. If the two colours look the same in a row, or the ratio falls near 1 to 1, people with that vision will struggle to tell them apart.

About the simulation

The simulation uses the matrices published by Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes in 2009 at full severity, applied to linear colour values. It is a good guide for design but only an approximation: real colour vision varies from person to person and many people have a milder form, called an anomaly rather than blindness.

This is a game, not a medical test. Screen colours change with brightness, night mode and the device. Only an optician or eye doctor can test colour vision.

Try these

Red and green is the classic trap. Try #d9382b next to #2e8b3d, then change green to blue and see the difference. See also a palette designed to stay distinct.
