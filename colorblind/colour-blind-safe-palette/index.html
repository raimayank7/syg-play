A colour-blind-safe palette

When you need several colours for a chart, a map or a diagram, the choice of colours decides whether all your readers can follow it. Masataka Okabe and Kei Ito, working on colour universal design, published a set of eight colours in 2008 that remain easy to tell apart for most kinds of colour vision. This page lists them and shows, by calculation, how each looks to people with three kinds of colour blindness.

How to use them

Use as many colours as the chart needs, in this order, and add a second cue where it is cheap to do so: different line styles, marker shapes, direct labels instead of a legend, or patterns on bars. The black is useful for text and axes. The yellow is bright and works well on dark backgrounds but poorly as text on white. Do not use these colours in a way that depends on telling the orange from the vermillion: they are close to each other for some viewers, so pair them with a label.

Check your own

Paste your own colours into the simulator and compare. The rule: if two colours must be told apart, make them differ in lightness as well as hue, because lightness differences survive every kind of colour blindness.
