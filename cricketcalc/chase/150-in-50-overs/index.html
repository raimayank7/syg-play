Chasing 150 in 50 overs: required rate and over-by-over table

What a target of 150 asks of a one-day innings: 3.00 runs an over from the first ball, 0.50 runs a ball, and how the rate changes as the innings goes on.

The basics

The target is 150, so the chasing side needs 150 runs to win and 149 to tie. Over 50 overs (300 balls) that is a required run rate of 3.00 an over from the start, which is 0.500 runs for every ball, or one run every 2.0 balls.

At 3.00 an over this is a gentle chase. Most of the work can be done with singles, twos and the occasional boundary, and a side that does not lose wickets in clumps should be able to stay ahead of the rate without taking risks. The danger in a low target is losing focus: three or four quiet overs in the middle can quietly raise the required rate to a level where one big over is needed.

In boundaries

If every run came in fours it would take 38 of them, and in sixes 25. In practice a team mixes singles, twos and boundaries. A useful picture: each over in which the side scores only 3 off the six balls is an over that keeps the required rate where it was, and every over below that raises it.

Par score and required rate as the innings goes on

"Par" here means the score at which the required rate is still exactly 3.00 an over. The table shows that score at several points of the chase, and the rate then needed if the side is 37 runs behind par, on par, or 37 ahead.
