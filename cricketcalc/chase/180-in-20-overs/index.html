Chasing 180 in 20 overs: required rate and over-by-over table

What a target of 180 asks of a T20 innings: 9.00 runs an over from the first ball, 1.50 runs a ball, and how the rate changes as the innings goes on.

The basics

The target is 180, so the chasing side needs 180 runs to win and 179 to tie. Over 20 overs (120 balls) that is a required run rate of 9.00 an over from the start, which is 1.500 runs for every ball, or one run every 0.7 balls.

9.00 an over is a demanding chase. Singles alone will not do: the side needs boundaries in most overs and cannot afford a run of dot balls. Wickets matter even more here, because a new batter needs time to settle and each lost over of tight bowling pushes the required rate up by half a run or more.

In boundaries

If every run came in fours it would take 45 of them, and in sixes 30. In practice a team mixes singles, twos and boundaries. A useful picture: each over in which the side scores only 9 off the six balls is an over that keeps the required rate where it was, and every over below that raises it.

Par score and required rate as the innings goes on

"Par" here means the score at which the required rate is still exactly 9.00 an over. The table shows that score at several points of the chase, and the rate then needed if the side is 15 runs behind par, on par, or 15 ahead.
