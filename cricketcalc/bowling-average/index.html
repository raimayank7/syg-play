Bowling average and strike rate calculator

A bowler's average is the runs they concede for every wicket they take. Their bowling strike rate is the number of balls they bowl for every wicket. Together with economy rate these three numbers describe a bowler fully: how tight, how often, and at what cost.

How to use it

  1. Type runs conceded and wickets taken.
  2. Optionally type balls bowled to also get the bowling strike rate and the economy rate.
  3. Press the button.

A worked example

A bowler takes 12 wickets for 360 runs from 288 balls. Average = 360 ÷ 12 = 30.00. Strike rate = 288 ÷ 12 = 24.0 balls per wicket. Economy = 360 ÷ 48 overs = 7.50. Check: economy × strike rate ÷ 6 = 7.5 × 24 ÷ 6 = 30, the average.

Each extra wicket lowers both numbers. Economy stays the same at 5.00 in this example, because the runs and balls do not change. That is why economy and average can tell different stories about the same spell.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Dividing wickets by runs. The average is runs per wicket, so runs go on top.
  • Using overs when the strike rate needs balls. 90 balls is 15 overs.

Questions people ask

Is a lower bowling average better?

Yes. It means fewer runs were conceded for each wicket.

What if the bowler took no wickets?

The average is not defined, because you cannot divide by zero wickets.

How do the three bowling numbers fit together?

Average = economy rate × strike rate ÷ 6, because runs per wicket equals runs per ball times balls per wicket.
