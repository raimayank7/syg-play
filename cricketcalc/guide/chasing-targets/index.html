How chasing a target works

A chase is the second innings of a limited-overs match: the team has a fixed number of balls to score one more run than the first innings total. Almost every sum in a chase comes down to balls, runs and the gap between the rate you need and the rate you are scoring.

Target and runs needed

The target is the first-innings total plus one. A team chasing 165 needs 165 runs to win and 164 to tie. After it has scored some runs, "runs needed" is the target minus the score.

Balls are what you really have

20 overs is 120 balls. Dot balls, singles and boundaries all use one ball each. A team needing 66 from 36 balls needs 1.83 runs a ball, which is 11 an over, and cannot get there with singles alone: it must find boundaries.

Required rate versus current rate

The required rate (see the calculator) is runs needed ÷ overs left. When it is higher than the current run rate the chasing side is behind the pace. It does not mean the game is lost: a short burst of boundaries can bring the required rate back in one over.

A chase in four steps

Suppose the target is 150 in 20 overs. Step 1: the required rate from the start is 150 ÷ 20 = 7.50. Step 2: after 10 overs the score is 62, so 88 are needed from 60 balls, a required rate of 8.80, higher than at the start. Step 3: a 14-run over brings it to 76, and 74 are needed from 54 balls, which is 8.22. Step 4: from there two boundaries in an over bring the rate under 8 again. A chase is a series of small corrections like this, which is why the required rate changes after every ball.
