Net run rate explained, step by step

Net run rate is a single number that says how fast a team has scored its runs compared with how fast it has let the opposition score theirs. It is used to separate teams that finish on equal points, and it is the reason a team with the same record as its rival can still miss the semi-final.

The formula

Net run rate = (total runs scored ÷ total overs faced) − (total runs conceded ÷ total overs bowled). The first part is the rate for, the second the rate against. Positive is good: it means the team scored faster than it conceded.

An easy one-match example

Your team scores 176 in 20 overs and the opponents score 168 in 20. Rate for = 176 ÷ 20 = 8.80. Rate against = 168 ÷ 20 = 8.40. Net run rate = 8.80 − 8.40 = +0.400. A narrow win gives a small positive figure; a heavy win gives a large one.

Over a whole league

You never average the match figures. You add all the runs scored and divide by all the overs faced, then do the same for runs conceded and overs bowled. This matters because a long, slow innings should weigh more than a short, quick one.

Chasing teams

A team that chases down a target in 15 overs has faced only 15 overs for its runs, so its rate for is high. This is why captains who are chasing often try to finish early, not only to win. The side that set the target, by the same sum, faced the full 20 overs.

Overs are written in balls

Remember that 17.2 overs is 17 overs and 2 balls, which is 17.333 overs when you divide. The calculator on this site does the change for you. The page on overs and balls explains why.

What it does not tell you

Net run rate does not say which team was the better side on the day, only how fast runs were scored. A team can have a high rate from one huge win and still have lost the other matches. It is a tie-breaker, not a ranking of quality.
