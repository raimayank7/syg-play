The all-out rule in net run rate

The most surprising rule in net run rate is that a team bowled out is treated as if it had faced all its overs. Here is why the rule exists and how much difference it makes.

The rule

If a team is all out before its overs are finished, the overs used for its innings in the net run rate sum are the full quota (20 in a T20 match, 50 in a one-day match), not the overs it actually batted. The same applies to the other side: if a team's bowlers bowled the opponents out, the team is treated as having bowled the full quota.

Numbers that show why

Team A scores 120 and is bowled out in 14 overs. Team B scores 140 in 20 overs. Without the rule, Team A would be said to have scored at 120 ÷ 14 = 8.57 an over, which makes a batting collapse look quick. With the rule it is 120 ÷ 20 = 6.00 an over. Team B scored at 140 ÷ 20 = 7.00.

Team A's net run rate from this match, with the rule: 6.00 − 7.00 = -1.000. Without the rule it would be 8.57 − 7.00 = --1.571, a much smaller deficit. The rule makes sure a collapse is properly counted against the team.

Why the rule is there

If an all-out innings was measured by the overs actually used, being bowled out cheaply and quickly would look like fast scoring. The full-quota rule removes any reward for collapsing.

The same rule works for the bowlers

When your bowlers dismiss the opposition, your "overs bowled" for that innings is also the full quota, even if they took only 14 overs. That shrinks the runs-per-over figure the other team gets, so a quick all-out win helps your rate against too.

Shortened and abandoned matches

Only matches with a result count. The ICC conditions for men’s one-day internationals say net run rate does not apply to a match declared a no result. Where Duckworth-Lewis-Stern is used and a match ends early with a result, the first team is credited with the second team’s par score (or, if DLS was applied earlier, with one run less than the second team’s target) over the overs the second team had. This calculator does not do DLS; type those figures in yourself if your tournament follows that rule, and check your own tournament’s playing conditions.

Source

ICC Men’s One-Day International Playing Conditions and ICC Men’s Twenty20 International Playing Conditions, both effective from October 2026, clause 16.10.2 (net run rate), read on icc-cricket.com on 13 November 2026 in the PDFs linked from the ICC page "Playing Conditions". Both say that a team all out in less than its full quota of overs is calculated on the full quota, that only matches with a result count, and give the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern credit rule described above. The IPL Playing Conditions 2026 (effective 1 March 2026, clause 16.12) say the same. Each tournament organiser publishes its own conditions.
