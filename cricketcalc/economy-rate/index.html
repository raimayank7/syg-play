Bowler economy rate calculator

Economy rate is the number of runs a bowler gives away per over. A lower rate means the bowler is keeping the batters quiet. The only trap is part overs: 3.4 overs is not 3.4 in the sum, it is 3 and two-thirds, and this calculator handles that.

How to use it

  1. Type the runs conceded.
  2. Type the overs bowled the cricket way: 3.4 means 3 overs and 4 balls. The digit after the dot can only be 0 to 5.
  3. The answer is runs divided by overs, with the overs turned into a proper decimal.

A worked example

A bowler bowls 3.4 overs and concedes 29 runs. The 3.4 overs are 3 × 6 + 4 = 22 balls, which is 3.667 overs. Economy = 29 ÷ 3.667 = 7.91. Using 3.4 directly would give 8.53, which is wrong.

The formula is runs conceded ÷ overs bowled, with overs turned into balls ÷ 6.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using 3.4 directly as the number of overs. It is 3 + 4/6 = 3.667 overs.
  • Including byes in the bowler's runs. Byes and leg byes are not charged to the bowler; wides and no-balls are.

Questions people ask

Do byes count against the bowler?

No. Byes and leg byes go to the team's extras but not to the bowler. Wides and no-balls are charged to the bowler.

Is economy rate more important than wickets?

They answer different questions. Economy shows how much a bowler restricts runs; wickets and bowling average show how often they take wickets.

Can a bowler's economy be below 4?

Yes, in one-day and Test cricket and sometimes in T20 spells, but how low is possible depends on the match.
