Batting strike rate calculator

A batter's strike rate tells you how quickly they score: how many runs they make for every 100 balls they face. A strike rate of 100 means a run a ball. This page gives the formula, an example and a calculator that also shows the figure in runs per over.

How to use it

  1. Type the runs the batter scored.
  2. Type the balls faced. Count balls, not overs: 4 overs is 24 balls.
  3. Press the button for the strike rate, runs a ball and runs an over.

A worked example

A batter makes 72 off 48 balls. Strike rate = 72 ÷ 48 × 100 = 150.00, which is 1.5 runs a ball or 9 runs an over. Another makes 30 off 40: 30 ÷ 40 × 100 = 75.00, three-quarters of a run a ball.

The formula is runs ÷ balls faced × 100.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using overs instead of balls. 30 runs from 5 overs is 30 off 30 balls, a strike rate of 100, not 30 ÷ 5.
  • Averaging strike rates of different innings. The correct career figure is total runs divided by total balls, times 100, not the mean of the individual rates.

Questions people ask

What is a good strike rate?

It depends on the format and the situation. In a short chase a high rate is needed; in a long innings a patient one may be right. Judge a rate against what the match required, not against one number.

Is strike rate the same as run rate?

No. Run rate is for a team, in runs per over. Strike rate is for a batter, in runs per 100 balls.

Do wides count as balls faced?

No. A wide is not counted as a ball faced by the batter. Scorers treat a few cases differently, a no-ball for one, so for official records use the count of the scorer or the tournament.
