Batting average calculator

The batting average is the runs a batter has scored for each time they were out. Not outs do not count as dismissals, which is why a player who stays unbeaten can have an average higher than their best score. Enter the totals and the calculator does the sum.

How to use it

  1. Type total runs scored.
  2. Type innings played, then the number of times the batter was not out.
  3. The average is runs divided by (innings minus not outs).

A worked example

Over 18 innings a batter scores 612 runs and is not out 3 times. Dismissals = 18 − 3 = 15. Average = 612 ÷ 15 = 40.80. Divide by all 18 innings instead and you get 34.00, which is not the batting average.

The formula is runs ÷ (innings − not outs).

How not outs change the average

A batter scores 600 runs in 20 innings. The more times they finish not out, the fewer dismissals there are to divide by, so the average rises even though the runs stay the same.

This is why a batter who is often not out in a chase can have an average that looks higher than their form. When you compare two batters, look at runs, average and strike rate together.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Dividing by innings instead of dismissals. That gives a lower figure that cricket does not use.
  • Counting an innings in which the batter did not bat. Only innings in which they came to the crease count.

Questions people ask

What if the batter was never out?

Then there is no dismissal to divide by and the average is not defined. Most scorers just show the runs.

Does "retired hurt" count as out?

No. A batter who retires hurt is recorded as not out, so that innings is a not out.

Is the average the same in every format?

The formula is the same everywhere. What counts as a good average changes a lot with format and pitch, so compare like with like.
