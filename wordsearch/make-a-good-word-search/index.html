How to make a good word search

A good word search is not the hardest one but the one that suits the people who will play it. Here is how to choose the words, the size and the level, with some of the sums worked out.

Choose the words

  • Pick words that belong together, so the puzzle has a theme and the list teaches something.
  • Use words of mixed lengths. A grid of all five-letter words is dull; a grid with a few long words and several short ones is more interesting.
  • Avoid words that are contained in other words on the list (for example CAT and CATTLE), unless you want the player to notice.
  • Spell carefully. The puzzle uses only the letters A to Z, so spaces and accents are dropped.

Choose the size

A word of length L can be placed in a grid of size n in (n − L + 1) × n positions across, and the same number down. The table shows how many starting positions there are for a six-letter word in grids of different sizes (across only).

GridStarting squares for a six-letter word across
8 by 824
10 by 1050
12 by 1284
15 by 15150
20 by 20300

The more positions, the harder the word is to find, because there are more places it could hide. A 15 by 15 grid is therefore much harder than a 10 by 10, even with the same words.

How full should the grid be?

The words use up a share of the squares and the rest is filler. With 14 words of average length seven in a 12 by 12 grid, the words take about 98 letters out of 144, but because words cross each other and share letters, the actual share is somewhat less. Fewer words and a bigger grid make an easier puzzle to scan but a harder one to finish.

Choose the level

See the levels explained. Use easy for beginners, medium for most people and hard for experienced puzzlers.

Test it

Before you print, play through the puzzle yourself and use Show answers to confirm every word is in the grid. The maker places each word in the grid and checks it, and tells you if a word would not fit.
