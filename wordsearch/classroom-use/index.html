Word Search

Word searches in the classroom

A word search is more than time filling. Used well, it makes children look closely at the spelling of key words, one letter at a time, which is exactly the habit that good spellers have. Teachers also like that it is quiet, needs no marking, and works for a whole class at once.

Seven uses

  1. Vocabulary at the start of a unit. Make a puzzle from the ten new words and ask children to guess the meanings from context after finding them.
  2. Spelling practice. Dictate the words and ask children to write them before they find them in the grid.
  3. Early finishers. Keep printed theme puzzles in a folder for children who finish their work early.
  4. Substitute lessons. A class that has played a word search before will settle at once, and the printed sheet needs no teacher knowledge.
  5. Clue mode. Our themed puzzles have a clue mode that replaces each word with its clue, so children think about meaning before they look for letters.
  6. Group work. Pairs find the words together and take turns holding the pencil.
  7. Home practice. Send the web address home so children can play on a phone.

Making your own

Use the word search maker. Type the words of your unit, one per line, choose the size and level, and print. A good rule is 10 to 14 words in a 12 by 12 grid for primary school and up to 20 words in a 15 by 15 grid for older children.

Choosing the level

Use easy (across and down) for children who are just learning to read; medium (also diagonal) from about the age of eight; hard (also backwards) for confident readers. Backwards words are the biggest jump in difficulty, because children have to read letters in an unfamiliar order.

Printing without wasting ink

The print view shows only the grid and word list in black on white. Choose black and white in your printer settings, and print two copies of the sheet per page for a class.
