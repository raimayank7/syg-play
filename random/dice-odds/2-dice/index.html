2 Dice Odds: Chances of Every Total

This page lists the chance of every possible total when you roll 2 fair six-sided dice. The numbers are worked out by counting every one of the 36 equally likely outcomes, not estimated by simulation. Use the dice roller to try it.

The headline numbers

  • Lowest total: 2. Highest total: 12.
  • Most likely total: 7, which comes up 16.67% of the time.
  • Average total: 7. Spread (standard deviation): 2.42.
  • Chance of rolling at least the average (7): 58.33%.
  • Rarest totals: 2.778% each.

Why seven is special

There are six ways to make 7 with two dice (1+6, 2+5, 3+4 and the same three the other way round) but only one way to make 2 or 12. That is why 7 comes up six times as often as 2 or 12, and why board games that ask for a particular total are easier with some numbers than others.

Where most rolls land

Nine rolls in ten (more precisely, at least 90% of them) give a total between 3 and 11. A total of 2 or more comes up 100.00% of the time. A total of 7 or more comes up 58.33% of the time. A total of 11 or more comes up 8.33% of the time. A total of 12 or more comes up 2.78% of the time. On average you would roll the single most likely total once in about 6.0 rolls, and the very top total once in 36 rolls. These are long-run averages: in a short session you will see runs of luck and bad luck, and that is normal.

Compared with 1 dice

Adding a die moves the average from 3.5 to 7 and widens the range of totals from 6 to 11. The likeliest total is 16.67% of rolls with 2 dice against 16.67% with 1 dice, so each extra die makes the result a little more predictable in relative terms.

How to read this

"Ways" counts the ordered outcomes of the dice that give the total, out of 36 possible. Divide to get the chance. Because the dice are fair and independent, each of the 36 outcomes has the same chance. A page like this answers practical questions: is it worth chasing a total of 11, or is aiming for 7 much better?
