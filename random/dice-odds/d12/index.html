d12 odds: the chance of rolling each result

A 12-sided die gives each face the same chance of 1 in 12, or 8.33%. This page shows what that means for the targets people ask about. Try rolls with the dice roller.

What the numbers mean

The average of a 12-sided die is 6.5, and a single roll can land anywhere from 1 to 12 with no pull towards the middle: unlike a total of several dice, one die has a flat distribution. After a miss, the next roll is exactly as likely as the first, because dice have no memory.

Over many rolls

A result with chance 1 in 12 is expected about once in 12 rolls, but it can take far longer or arrive at once. In 10 rolls you will see the top face at least once 58.11% of the time, in 20 rolls 82.45% and in 50 rolls 98.71%. The chance of never rolling a 1 in 10 rolls is 41.89%.

Rolling twice

If you roll twice and keep the better result (often called advantage in role-playing games), the chance of reaching a target is 1 − (1 − p)², where p is the chance on one roll. That is the right-hand column.
