3 Dice Odds: Chances of Every Total

This page lists the chance of every possible total when you roll 3 fair six-sided dice. The numbers are worked out by counting every one of the 216 equally likely outcomes, not estimated by simulation. Use the dice roller to try it.

The headline numbers

  • Lowest total: 3. Highest total: 18.
  • Most likely total: 10, which comes up 12.50% of the time.
  • Average total: 10.5. Spread (standard deviation): 2.96.
  • Chance of rolling at least the average (10.5): 50.00%.
  • Rarest totals: 0.463% each.

Three dice and the bell shape

With three dice the totals already form the rounded bell shape that gets sharper with more dice. The two middle totals, 10 and 11, are each 12.50% of rolls, and the extreme totals 3 and 18 are each 0.46%.

Where most rolls land

Nine rolls in ten (more precisely, at least 90% of them) give a total between 6 and 15. A total of 3 or more comes up 100.00% of the time. A total of 10 or more comes up 62.50% of the time. A total of 15 or more comes up 9.26% of the time. A total of 18 or more comes up 0.46% of the time. On average you would roll the single most likely total once in about 8.0 rolls, and the very top total once in 216 rolls. These are long-run averages: in a short session you will see runs of luck and bad luck, and that is normal.

Compared with 2 dice

Adding a die moves the average from 7 to 10.5 and widens the range of totals from 11 to 16. The likeliest total is 12.50% of rolls with 3 dice against 16.67% with 2 dice, so each extra die makes the result a little more predictable in relative terms.

How to read this

"Ways" counts the ordered outcomes of the dice that give the total, out of 216 possible. Divide to get the chance. Because the dice are fair and independent, each of the 216 outcomes has the same chance. A page like this answers practical questions: is it worth chasing a total of 17, or is aiming for 10 much better?
