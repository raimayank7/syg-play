6 Dice Odds: Chances of Every Total

This page lists the chance of every possible total when you roll 6 fair six-sided dice. The numbers are worked out by counting every one of the 46,656 equally likely outcomes, not estimated by simulation. Use the dice roller to try it.

The headline numbers

  • Lowest total: 6. Highest total: 36.
  • Most likely total: 21, which comes up 9.28% of the time.
  • Average total: 21. Spread (standard deviation): 4.18.
  • Chance of rolling at least the average (21): 54.64%.
  • Rarest totals: 0.002% each.

Where most rolls land

Nine rolls in ten (more precisely, at least 90% of them) give a total between 14 and 28. A total of 6 or more comes up 100.00% of the time. A total of 21 or more comes up 54.64% of the time. A total of 28 or more comes up 6.08% of the time. A total of 36 or more comes up 0.00% of the time. On average you would roll the single most likely total once in about 10.8 rolls, and the very top total once in 46,656 rolls. These are long-run averages: in a short session you will see runs of luck and bad luck, and that is normal.

Compared with 5 dice

Adding a die moves the average from 17.5 to 21 and widens the range of totals from 26 to 31. The likeliest total is 9.28% of rolls with 6 dice against 10.03% with 5 dice, so each extra die makes the result a little more predictable in relative terms.

How to read this

"Ways" counts the ordered outcomes of the dice that give the total, out of 46656 possible. Divide to get the chance. Because the dice are fair and independent, each of the 46656 outcomes has the same chance. A page like this answers practical questions: is it worth chasing a total of 35, or is aiming for 21 much better?
