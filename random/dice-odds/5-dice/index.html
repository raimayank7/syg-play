5 Dice Odds: Chances of Every Total

This page lists the chance of every possible total when you roll 5 fair six-sided dice. The numbers are worked out by counting every one of the 7,776 equally likely outcomes, not estimated by simulation. Use the dice roller to try it.

The headline numbers

  • Lowest total: 5. Highest total: 30.
  • Most likely total: 17, which comes up 10.03% of the time.
  • Average total: 17.5. Spread (standard deviation): 3.82.
  • Chance of rolling at least the average (17.5): 50.00%.
  • Rarest totals: 0.013% each.

Where most rolls land

Nine rolls in ten (more precisely, at least 90% of them) give a total between 11 and 24. A total of 5 or more comes up 100.00% of the time. A total of 17 or more comes up 60.03% of the time. A total of 24 or more comes up 5.88% of the time. A total of 30 or more comes up 0.01% of the time. On average you would roll the single most likely total once in about 10.0 rolls, and the very top total once in 7,776 rolls. These are long-run averages: in a short session you will see runs of luck and bad luck, and that is normal.

Compared with 4 dice

Adding a die moves the average from 14 to 17.5 and widens the range of totals from 21 to 26. The likeliest total is 10.03% of rolls with 5 dice against 11.27% with 4 dice, so each extra die makes the result a little more predictable in relative terms.

How to read this

"Ways" counts the ordered outcomes of the dice that give the total, out of 7776 possible. Divide to get the chance. Because the dice are fair and independent, each of the 7776 outcomes has the same chance. A page like this answers practical questions: is it worth chasing a total of 29, or is aiming for 17 much better?
