One Die Odds: Chance of Each Number

This page lists the chance of every possible total when you roll 1 fair six-sided die. The numbers are worked out by counting every one of the 6 equally likely outcomes, not estimated by simulation. Use the dice roller to try it.

The headline numbers

  • Lowest total: 1. Highest total: 6.
  • Most likely total: 1, which comes up 16.67% of the time.
  • Average total: 3.5. Spread (standard deviation): 1.71.
  • Chance of rolling at least the average (3.5): 50.00%.
  • Rarest totals: 16.667% each.

Where most rolls land

Nine rolls in ten (more precisely, at least 90% of them) give a total between 1 and 6. A total of 1 or more comes up 100.00% of the time. A total of 3 or more comes up 66.67% of the time. A total of 6 or more comes up 16.67% of the time. On average you would roll the single most likely total once in about 6.0 rolls, and the very top total once in 6 rolls. These are long-run averages: in a short session you will see runs of luck and bad luck, and that is normal.

How to read this

"Ways" counts the ordered outcomes of the dice that give the total, out of 6 possible. Divide to get the chance. Because the dice are fair and independent, each of the 6 outcomes has the same chance. A page like this answers practical questions: is it worth chasing a total of 6, or is aiming for 1 much better?
