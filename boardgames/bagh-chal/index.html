How to play Bagh-Chal (tigers and goats)

Players
2
You need
The board (easily drawn), 4 tiger pieces and 20 goat pieces
A game takes
15 to 30 minutes
Aim
Tigers: capture five goats. Goats: leave the tigers no move

Bagh-Chal means "the tiger's move". It is the traditional board game of Nepal, and games of the same family, in which a few strong hunters face a crowd of weak prey, are played all over South Asia under names such as Bagh Bakri, Sher Bakar and Aadu Puli Attam. The two sides are unequal on purpose: one player has four tigers that can kill, the other has twenty goats that can only crowd. Played well it is finely balanced, and it is a wonderful game for showing children that cooperation can beat strength.

The board

The board is a square of 25 points in five rows of five. Lines join each point to its neighbours along the rows and columns, and diagonal lines run corner to corner and round a diamond that joins the middles of the four sides. Pieces stand on the points and move along the lines. Not every point has diagonals: look at the lines, not the squares.

Setting up

Place the four tigers on the four corner points. The twenty goats start off the board, in the goat player's hand.

How to play

The goat player moves first, and the players then take turns.

The goats

  • While goats remain in hand, the goat player's turn is to place one goat on any empty point. Goats on the board may not move during this stage.
  • When all twenty goats have been placed, the goat player's turn is to move one goat along a line to a neighbouring empty point.
  • Goats never jump and never capture.

The tigers

  • On each turn the tiger player moves one tiger. Tigers move from the very first turn, while the goats are still being placed.
  • A tiger may step along a line to a neighbouring empty point.
  • Or a tiger may capture a goat by jumping over it, along a line, to the empty point directly beyond. The goat is removed from the game.
  • A tiger may jump only one goat in a turn; there are no double jumps. A tiger cannot jump over another tiger, and capturing is not compulsory.

Winning

  • The tigers win when they have captured five goats.
  • The goats win when no tiger can move: every tiger is hemmed in, with no empty neighbouring point and no possible jump.

To stop a game going round in circles, many players add a rule that a position may not be repeated: you may not make a move that brings back an arrangement that has already occurred.

Tips for the goats

  • Edges first. A goat on the edge of the board cannot be jumped from outside. Fill the edges early and work inwards.
  • Stand in pairs. A goat with another goat directly behind it, on the same line as the tiger, cannot be jumped, because the landing point is taken.
  • Never place a goat next to a tiger with an empty point behind it. Check every line through the point before you place.
  • Do not hurry. You have twenty goats and can afford to lose four. Use the placing stage to build a solid wall; the trapping comes later.
  • Sometimes a sacrifice is right: giving one goat to pull a tiger into a corner where it can be shut in.

Tips for the tigers

  • Stay apart and stay central. Tigers bunched in a corner are easy to trap. A tiger in the middle has the most lines to move along.
  • Threaten two goats at once. The goat player can protect only one each turn.
  • Capture early. The placing stage is your best hunting time, while the goats are few and scattered. Once the wall is built, kills are rare.
  • Keep empty points behind the goats. If the goats fill the board, your tigers will run out of moves.

Play it anywhere

The board can be drawn on paper, chalked on a floor or scratched in sand. Use four large stones for tigers and twenty small ones for goats. Swap sides after each game, since the two roles feel quite different, and count a pair of games as one match. You can print the board from printable boards.
