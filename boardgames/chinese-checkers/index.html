How to play Chinese checkers

Players
2, 3, 4 or 6
You need
A star-shaped board with 121 holes, 10 marbles or pegs per player
A game takes
20 to 40 minutes
Aim
Be first to move all ten marbles into the opposite point of the star

Chinese checkers is a race game with no dice and no captures. Each player tries to move ten marbles from one point of a six-pointed star to the point directly opposite, hopping over other marbles to travel fast. Despite the name, the game did not come from China and it is not a form of checkers; it is a star-shaped version of an older Western game called Halma, and the "Chinese" name was a selling idea. It has been a family favourite in India for generations because children and adults can play on level terms.

The board

Setting up

  • Two players: take opposite points. (Some play with fifteen marbles each, filling an extra row.)
  • Three players: use every second point; each player's target is the empty point opposite.
  • Four players: two pairs of opposite points.
  • Six players: all six points.

Each player fills their home triangle with ten marbles of one colour. Five players is awkward, because one player would have an empty target while the others must wait for an opponent to leave theirs.

How to move

Players take turns, going round the board. On your turn you move one marble, in one of two ways:

  1. A step: move the marble to any of the six neighbouring holes, if it is empty.
  2. A hop: jump over one neighbouring marble, of any colour including your own, into the empty hole directly beyond it in a straight line. After a hop, the same marble may hop again, and again, changing direction freely, for as long as hops are available. You may stop whenever you like.

You cannot combine a step and a hop in one turn. Marbles that are hopped over are not removed; nothing is ever captured in this game.

Where marbles may go

A marble may travel anywhere in the central hexagon and in your own home and target triangles. In the usual rule, it may pass through the other points of the star during a chain of hops but may not come to rest in a triangle that is neither its home nor its target. Once a marble has entered its target triangle it may move about inside it but may not leave.

Winning

The first player to fill the opposite triangle with all ten of their marbles wins. Others may play on for places.

The blocking problem

A spiteful player can leave one marble sitting in their home triangle so that the opponent can never fill it. Rule books deal with this in different ways. The simplest fair rule: if a hole in your target triangle is occupied by an opponent's marble that could have moved away, you may swap one of your marbles with it when you reach it; or, more simply, you win when every hole in the target that is not occupied by an opponent holds one of your marbles. Agree on this before you start.

Tips

  • Build a ladder. A line of marbles with one-hole gaps lets the marbles behind hop the whole length in one turn. In the opening, step a few marbles forward to form the rungs, then send the rest over them.
  • Use everyone's marbles. You can hop over any colour. Look for long chains through the crowd in the middle.
  • Do not leave stragglers. The game is usually lost by the last one or two marbles left behind with nothing to hop over. Bring the back marbles forward while the ladder still exists.
  • Fill the far end of the target first. Marbles that stop at the entrance of the target triangle block your own later arrivals.
  • Break the opponent's ladder by stepping a marble into one of its gaps, when it also helps you.
  • Count moves in the endgame. With two players the last stage is a pure race: each needless step is a turn lost.

Variations

  • Long hops: a marble may jump over a marble any number of empty holes away, landing the same distance beyond it, if nothing else is in the way. Very fast, and harder to see.
  • Teams: with four or six players, partners sitting opposite each other win when both have filled their targets.
  • Two colours each: with two or three players, each controls two sets of marbles and must bring both home.
