Rules of classic board games, simply explained

16 traditional games with clear rules, drawn boards, the usual house-rule arguments settled, and tips to play better.

  • ChessTwo armies of sixteen pieces. Trap the enemy king.2 players · board and pieces · age 6 and up
  • Draughts (checkers)Jump and capture on the dark squares; crown your kings.2 players · chess board and 24 discs · age 6 and up
  • LudoRace four tokens round the cross and home. Roll a six to start.2 to 4 players · board, dice, tokens · age 5 and up
  • Snakes and laddersClimb the ladders, slide down the snakes, first to 100 wins.2 or more players · board and one dice · age 4 and up
  • CarromFlick the striker to pocket your nine pieces and the red queen.2 or 4 players · carrom board, pieces, striker, powder · age 7 and up
  • Nine men's morrisMake rows of three ("mills") to remove enemy pieces.2 players · drawn board, 9 counters each · age 7 and up
  • BackgammonRace fifteen checkers home with two dice; hit and block on the way.2 players · backgammon board, 30 checkers, 2 dice · age 8 and up
  • Chinese checkersHop your ten marbles across the star into the opposite point.2 to 6 players · star board and marbles · age 6 and up
  • ReversiTrap rows of enemy discs between yours and flip them to your colour.2 players · 8 by 8 board, 64 two-coloured discs · age 7 and up
  • Five in a row (gomoku)Take turns placing stones; the first unbroken row of five wins.2 players · squared paper and pens, or a board and stones · age 6 and up
  • Bagh-Chal (tigers and goats)Four tigers hunt twenty goats; the goats try to trap the tigers.2 players · drawn board, 4 + 20 counters · age 7 and up
  • Mancala (sowing game)Scoop seeds from a pit and sow them round the board; capture the most.2 players · board with 12 pits and 2 stores, 48 seeds · age 6 and up
  • DominoesMatch the ends of the tiles; be first to play your whole hand.2 to 4 players · a set of 28 dominoes · age 5 and up
  • Noughts and crosses (tic-tac-toe)Three in a row on a three by three grid. Simple, and a first lesson in strategy.2 players · pen and paper · age 4 and up
  • Dots and boxesJoin dots to close boxes. Simple to start, sly at the end.2 or more players · pen and paper · age 6 and up
  • Battleships on paperHide a fleet on a grid and take turns calling shots to sink the other's.2 players · squared paper and pens · age 7 and up

Choose a game

Not sure what to play? Think about who is at the table.

  • Small children (4 to 7): snakes and ladders needs no decisions at all; ludo adds a few; noughts and crosses and dominoes need only matching and counting.
  • The whole family, mixed ages: ludo, carrom and Chinese checkers let a child beat a grandparent now and then.
  • Two players who like to think: chess, draughts, reversi, nine men's morris and Bagh-Chal.
  • Luck and skill mixed: backgammon.
  • Nothing but paper and a pen: dots and boxes, five in a row, battleships, noughts and crosses.
  • Number practice in disguise: mancala.

What you will find on each page

Every game page gives what you need, how to set up, the rules in order, and the points that families most often argue about, stated plainly. Where a game is played in more than one way, we give the common rule and name the variations, so that you can agree before you start. The bigger games have extra pages: how the chess pieces move, ludo house rules, carrom fouls and more. Every board on this site was drawn for it, and several can be printed.

Games that belong to everyone

The games here are traditional. Many are hundreds of years old, some thousands, and several were born in India: chess grew from the old Indian game of chaturanga, ludo from Pachisi, snakes and ladders from Moksha Patam, and carrom from the Indian subcontinent. Nobody owns them, and anyone may make a board and play. We have left out modern games that belong to a company.

Three rules for every game

  1. Agree the rules before the first move. Most quarrels come from two people who learnt different versions.
  2. Swap who goes first. In nearly every game the first player has an edge.
  3. Play for fun. We suggest never playing for money. With small children, choose games that have a share of luck, so that the youngest player wins honestly now and then.
