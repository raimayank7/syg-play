How to play chess: the rules

Players
2
You need
A board of 64 squares and 32 pieces
A game takes
10 minutes to several hours
Aim
Checkmate: trap the opponent's king

Chess is a game for two players on a board of 64 squares. Each player commands sixteen pieces and tries to trap the other's king. There is no dice and nothing hidden: both players see everything, and the better plan wins. The rules can be learnt in an afternoon. This page gives the whole game in outline; the pages linked from it explain each part with diagrams.

The board

The board has eight rows and eight columns of squares, coloured light and dark in turn. Place it so that each player has a light square in the right-hand corner. The rows across are called ranks and are numbered 1 to 8 from White's side. The columns are called files and are lettered a to h from White's left. Every square has a name made of its file and rank: the bottom-left corner for White is a1, the top-right is h8.

Setting up

  1. Put the eight pawns on the second rank in front of you.
  2. Put a rook on each corner.
  3. Next to each rook, a knight.
  4. Next to each knight, a bishop.
  5. The queen goes on the remaining square of her own colour: white queen on the light square, black queen on the dark square.
  6. The king takes the last square, beside the queen.

Checked correctly, the two kings face each other on the e-file and the two queens on the d-file.

How the game is played

  • White moves first, then the players move in turn, one move each. You may not pass.
  • A move takes one piece from its square to another square. (Castling, which moves the king and a rook together, is the one exception.)
  • If you move a piece onto a square held by an enemy piece, you capture it: the enemy piece is taken off the board for good and yours takes its place. You can never capture your own pieces, and capturing is never compulsory.
  • No piece may jump over another, except the knight.

Each kind of piece moves in its own way. See how the pieces move.

Check and checkmate

When a king is attacked, that is, an enemy piece could capture it on the next move, the king is in check. A player whose king is in check must get out of check at once: by moving the king, by capturing the attacker, or by placing a piece in between. You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check.

If the king is in check and there is no way out, it is checkmate. The game ends there and the player who gave checkmate wins. The king is never actually taken.

A game can also end in a draw, for instance by stalemate, when the player to move has no legal move but is not in check. All the ways a game can end are on check, checkmate and draws.

Three special moves

Castling tucks the king away and brings a rook out in one move. En passant is a special pawn capture. Promotion turns a pawn that reaches the far side into a queen or another piece. They are explained on special moves.

Good manners and the touch-move rule

In serious play, if you deliberately touch one of your pieces you must move it, if it has a legal move; if you touch an enemy piece you must capture it, if you can. A move is finished when you let go of the piece. To straighten a piece on its square, say "I adjust" first. At home, agree before you start whether you are playing touch-move; it avoids quarrels and teaches you to think before reaching.

What the pieces are worth

The pieces are not equal. As a rough guide used by players everywhere, count a pawn as 1, a knight or a bishop as 3, a rook as 5 and a queen as 9. The king has no number: lose him and you lose the game. The guide tells you which exchanges are good: giving a knight for a rook is usually a gain; giving a queen for a rook is usually a disaster.

Questions people ask

Who moves first in chess?

White always moves first. Players usually decide who takes White by hiding a pawn of each colour in closed hands and letting the opponent choose a hand.

Can the king capture a piece?

Yes. The king captures like any other piece, one square in any direction, provided the square it lands on is not attacked by another enemy piece.

Can a pawn move backwards?

No. Pawns are the only pieces that can never move or capture backwards.

Do you have to say "check"?

No. The rules do not require it, though it is a courtesy in friendly games. In tournament play it is not said.

What happens if only the two kings are left?

The game is a draw, because neither side can ever give checkmate.
