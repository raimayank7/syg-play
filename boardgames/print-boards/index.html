Printable game boards

Nine boards drawn for this site. Tap Print under a board to print just that board on one sheet.

Each board below is a drawing made for this site. Tap Print under the one you want: only that board is printed, enlarged to fill a page. In the print window, choose A4 paper and, for the square boards, portrait layout. For a longer-lasting board, print on thick paper or paste the sheet on to card. Colour is needed only for the ludo board; the others print well in black and white.

Making a board that lasts

  • Paste the printed sheet on the lid of a shoe box or a piece of an old carton, and trim the edges.
  • Cover it with clear tape or slip it into a transparent file pocket; then it wipes clean and survives a spill.
  • For the paper games, a sheet in a plastic pocket with a whiteboard marker can be used again and again.
  • Keep the pieces in a small tin or a zip pouch taped to the back of the board.

Bigger boards

For a floor-size game, print the board as a guide and copy it on to chart paper with a ruler, or chalk it on a terrace. The make your own snakes and ladders page explains how to design a board that plays well.

For schools and clubs

You may print and photocopy these boards freely for classrooms, libraries, clubs, camps and homes. A set of printed boards and a bag of bottle caps is a games corner that costs almost nothing. We ask only that the boards are not sold.

Why print at all?

A phone can play most of these games, but a board on a table does something a screen does not: it puts two people face to face, lets a third watch and a fourth give bad advice. That, more than the rules, is what these games have been for through all their long history.
