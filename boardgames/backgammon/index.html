How to play backgammon

Players
2
You need
A backgammon board, 15 checkers each, two dice (a doubling cube is optional)
A game takes
10 to 30 minutes
Aim
Be first to move all your checkers home and off the board

Backgammon is a race with fighting. Each player moves fifteen checkers round the board according to two dice, and tries to bring them all home and take them off before the opponent does. On the way you can hit enemy checkers and block their path. Luck decides a single game; skill decides a long series. Backgammon in its present form took shape in seventeenth-century England, but it belongs to the family of "tables" games, which is at least sixteen hundred years old.

The board

The board has 24 narrow triangles called points, in four quarters of six. Number them 1 to 24 from your own side: points 1 to 6 are your home board, and 19 to 24 are your opponent's home board. The strip down the middle is the bar. Your checkers travel from the high numbers to the low ones; your opponent's travel the other way, so the two armies pass through each other.

Setting up

Each player places, counting from their own side: two checkers on the 24-point, five on the 13-point, three on the 8-point and five on the 6-point. The two set-ups mirror each other.

Starting

Each player rolls one dice. The higher number goes first and uses the two numbers just rolled as the first move. If they are equal, roll again. After that, players take turns to roll both dice.

Moving

  • The two dice are two separate moves. With a 5 and a 3 you may move one checker five points and another three, or one checker five and then three (or three and then five), provided the point it touches in between is open.
  • A checker may land only on an open point: one that is empty, holds your own checkers, or holds exactly one enemy checker. A point with two or more enemy checkers is closed to you.
  • Doubles are played twice. A roll of 4 and 4 gives you four moves of four.
  • You must use both numbers if you legally can. If only one can be played, you must play it; if either one could be played but not both, you must play the larger. If neither can be played, you lose the turn.

Hitting and the bar

A single checker on a point is a blot. If you land on an enemy blot, it is hit and placed on the bar. A player with a checker on the bar must bring it back into play before moving anything else. It re-enters in the opponent's home board, on the point matching a dice roll: a 1 enters on the 24-point, a 2 on the 23-point, and so on to a 6 on the 19-point, and only if that point is open. If both numbers are blocked, the turn is lost.

Bearing off

When all fifteen of your checkers are in your home board, you begin to bear off, taking them from the board. A roll of 4 removes a checker from the 4-point. If there is no checker on that point, you must move a checker from a higher point; if there is none higher, you remove a checker from the highest point that has one. If one of your checkers is hit during bearing off, it must go round again and reach your home board before you take off any more.

Winning and scoring

  • The first player to bear off all fifteen checkers wins the game: one point.
  • If the loser has not borne off a single checker, it is a gammon, worth double.
  • If the loser has borne off none and still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board, it is a backgammon, worth triple.

Friends usually play a match to an agreed number of points, such as five or seven.

The doubling cube

Serious players use a cube marked 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 to raise the stakes. Before rolling, a player who feels ahead may offer to double the value of the game. The opponent either refuses, and loses the game at its current value, or accepts and takes the cube; from then on only the player holding the cube may offer the next double. In match play, the Crawford rule says that in the first game after one player comes within a point of winning the match, the cube may not be used. Beginners can leave the cube in the box.

First ideas of strategy

  • Make points. Two checkers together are safe and block the enemy. Six points in a row is a wall no checker can cross.
  • Do not leave blots within six points in front of enemy checkers without a reason; the nearer, the likelier a hit.
  • Bring your two back checkers out before they are trapped.
  • If you are ahead in the race, race. If you are behind, stay back, hold a point in the enemy home board and wait for a shot.

We suggest playing for points and pride, never for money.

Questions people ask

How many checkers does each player have in backgammon?

Fifteen.

What happens when you roll doubles?

You play the number four times instead of twice.

Can you pass in backgammon?

No. If a legal move exists you must make it. You lose your turn only when no number can be played.
