Ludo strategy: which token to move

The dice decides how far, but you decide which token. Over a full game each player makes dozens of such choices, and a player who chooses well wins far more than a fair share. None of this needs arithmetic at the table; a few facts about the dice and a few habits are enough.

The one fact to remember: distance and danger

An enemy token can capture you this turn only if it rolls the exact distance between you. With one dice:

  • A token that is 1 to 6 squares behind you has a one-in-six chance of landing on you with its next roll, a little more when the rule of rolling again after a six is counted.
  • A token 7 to 12 squares behind you needs a six followed by the right number: about one chance in thirty-six. That is nearly safe.
  • A token more than 12 squares behind you is no threat this turn.
  • A token in front of you cannot hurt you at all; tokens only move forward.

So the dangerous zone is the six squares in front of any enemy token. Before you move, look where each of your tokens would land, and count how many enemy tokens would be within six squares behind it. Two enemies within six squares behind you means about a one-in-three chance of being sent home.

Habits that win

1. Capture when you can

A capture costs the enemy every square that token has travelled, and they then need a six to return. It is almost always the best move available, especially against a token that is far round the board. The exception: do not capture if your capturing token would land in front of two or three enemies and the victim had hardly started.

2. Bring out a second token early

With one token on the track you have no choices, and every roll must be used on it, however bad the landing square. With two or three out, you can pick the safest move each turn. Use your early sixes to bring tokens out. But do not bring out a token onto the start square if an enemy sits within six squares behind it and safe squares are not in use.

3. Run when you are in the danger zone; stroll when you are not

If one of your tokens stands within six squares in front of an enemy, moving it out of range is usually better than advancing a token that is already safe. If none of your tokens is in danger, advance the one that lands on the safest square.

4. Use the safe squares

If you play with star squares, plan to hop from one safe square to the next. A token resting on a safe square can wait there while your other tokens take the rolls. Do not leave a safe square for an ordinary square just in front of an enemy without a reason.

5. Make blocks, and put them where they hurt

Two of your tokens together cannot be captured. If your house rules make a block impassable, a block placed just beyond an opponent's start square, or before the entrance to their home column, can hold them up for many turns. Keep enough other tokens free that you are not forced to break the block by a roll you cannot use elsewhere.

6. Hunt from behind

The safest place on the board is just behind an enemy token: it cannot move backwards to capture you, and you threaten it every turn. Trailing an enemy by three or four squares puts pressure on them to run.

7. Think about your own start square

Enemy tokens passing your yard are in danger whenever you roll a six, because your new token lands on the start square. If an enemy token rests there and it is not a safe square in your rules, a six captures it. Equally, do not park your own tokens on other players' start squares.

8. Do not rush one token home while the others sleep

A token that reaches home can no longer capture, block or give you choices. A player with one token home and three in the yard is usually behind a player with three tokens spread round the board. Bring your tokens round together when you can.

9. In the home column, save your small numbers

Because you need the exact number to finish, a token one square from home waits for a one. If you have a choice, use small rolls to finish tokens that are close and large rolls on tokens still on the track. Try not to leave two tokens both needing the same small number.

10. Watch the leader

With three or four players, the game is partly politics. If one player is far ahead, the others gain by capturing that player's tokens before each other's. If you are the leader, keep your tokens on safe squares and away from everyone.

Which token? A quick order of thought

  1. Can I capture? Is it safe enough? Do it.
  2. Can I bring a token home, or into the home column? Usually do it.
  3. Is one of my tokens in danger? Move it to a safe square or out of range.
  4. Have I rolled a six with tokens still in the yard? Usually bring one out.
  5. Otherwise, make the move that leaves all my tokens with the fewest enemies within six squares behind them.

Luck still matters

The best player at the table will still lose many games of ludo, and that is part of its charm: a six-year-old can beat a grandparent. Play the odds, enjoy the upsets, and never blame the dice aloud more than once a game.

See also the rules and the house rules, which change some of this advice: with a bonus roll for captures, for example, capturing is worth even more.
