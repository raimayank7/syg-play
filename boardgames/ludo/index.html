How to play ludo: the rules

Players
2 to 4
You need
A ludo board, one dice, four tokens of each colour
A game takes
30 to 60 minutes
Aim
Be first to bring all four tokens home

Ludo is a race. Each player has four tokens that must travel once round the board and up their own coloured column to the centre. The dice decides how far you move; you decide which token to move, and that choice is the whole skill of the game. Ludo is played in almost every Indian home, and almost every home has its own extra rules. This page gives the standard game first. The usual variations, and the arguments they cause, have a page of their own.

The board

  • Yards: the four large coloured corners. Your four tokens wait here at the start.
  • The track: the path of squares round the cross, shared by everyone. Tokens travel clockwise.
  • Start square: the coloured square just outside each yard, where that colour's tokens enter the track.
  • Home column: the five coloured squares leading to the middle. Only tokens of that colour may enter it.
  • Home: the triangle of your colour in the centre.
  • Star squares: many boards mark safe squares with a star. They are a common extra rule, explained below.

Starting

  1. Each player takes a colour and puts four tokens in the matching yard. With two players, take opposite colours.
  2. Everyone rolls the dice once. The highest roll goes first, and play passes clockwise.

Your turn

  1. Roll the dice.
  2. To bring a token out of the yard you need a six. A six lets you place one token on your start square. If you have no token on the track and do not roll a six, your turn is over.
  3. Otherwise, move one of your tokens on the track forward by exactly the number rolled. If you have several tokens out, choose any one of them. You must move if a legal move exists, even if you would prefer not to.
  4. A six gives you another roll. After using the six (to bring out a token or to move six squares), roll again.
  5. Three sixes in a row: the third six is not played and your turn ends at once.

Capturing

If your token lands, by exact count, on a square occupied by a single token of another colour, that token is captured and goes back to its yard. Its owner will need a six to bring it out again. You do not capture by passing over a token, only by landing on it.

Blocks

Two tokens of the same colour on one square form a block. An opponent's token cannot land on a block; in the standard rules a token that would land there by exact count is itself sent back to its yard, so players count carefully. Whether a block also stops tokens from passing is a matter of house rules.

Going home

  • When a token has gone all the way round and reaches the entrance to its own home column, it turns up the column instead of starting a second lap.
  • Tokens in the home column are safe: no other colour can enter it.
  • To reach the home triangle you need the exact number. If the roll is too big, that token cannot move; move another token if you can, or pass.

Winning

The first player to get all four tokens home wins. The others usually play on for second and third place.

Safe squares (very common)

On boards with stars, a token on a star square, or on any start square, cannot be captured. Tokens of different colours can share such a square. This rule is so widespread in India, and built into so many printed boards and phone apps, that many players think of it as standard. Agree before the game whether you are using it.

Where the game comes from

Ludo is a simplified descendant of Pachisi, the old Indian cross-and-circle game played with cowrie shells on a cloth board. In England a dice version was sold under the name "Ludo" (Latin for "I play") in 1896 and later patented. The pattern came back to India in that form, which is why the modern game is known here by a Latin name.

Questions people ask

Do you need a six to start in ludo?

Yes, in the standard rules a token leaves the yard only on a six. Some families also allow a one; that is a house rule.

What happens if you roll three sixes in a row?

The third six is not played and the turn passes to the next player. The first two sixes stand.

Can two tokens of different colours share a square?

Only on a safe square, if you are playing with safe squares. On an ordinary square, the token that arrives captures the one already there.

Do you have to roll the exact number to get home?

Yes. A roll that would take the token past the home triangle cannot be used for that token.

Can I choose not to move?

No. If any of your tokens can legally move the number rolled, you must move one of them.
