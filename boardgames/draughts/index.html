How to play draughts (checkers)

Players
2
You need
A board of 64 squares, 12 dark and 12 light discs
A game takes
10 to 30 minutes
Aim
Capture or block all the opponent's pieces

Draughts, known as checkers in North America, is one of the oldest and simplest board games: flat discs move diagonally on the dark squares of a chess board and capture by jumping. The rules below are those of English draughts, the form played on the 8 by 8 board in Britain, India and America. Other countries play related games on larger boards with somewhat different rules; the main differences are listed at the end.

Setting up

Place the board so that each player has a dark square in the left-hand corner nearest them. Each player has twelve pieces, called men, and puts them on the twelve dark squares of the three rows nearest to them. The two middle rows start empty. All play takes place on the dark squares; the light squares are never used.

Moving

  • The player with the darker pieces moves first. Then the players take turns.
  • A man moves one square diagonally forward onto an empty dark square. Men never move backwards.

Capturing

  • If an enemy piece is on the next diagonal square in front of your man, and the square directly beyond it is empty, your man jumps over it and lands on the empty square. The jumped piece is captured and taken off the board.
  • Capturing is compulsory. If you can jump, you must. You may not make an ordinary move instead.
  • If, after a jump, the same piece can jump again, it must go on jumping in the same turn, changing direction if need be, until it has no further jump. All the pieces it jumped are removed.
  • If you have a choice between different captures, you may take whichever you like; you are not obliged to choose the one that takes most pieces. But whichever you start, you must finish the whole series of jumps.
  • A man jumps only forwards.

Kings

When a man reaches the far row of the board, it is crowned and becomes a king. Mark it by placing a second disc on top. Crowning ends the move: a man that reaches the last row in the middle of a series of jumps stops there, is crowned, and cannot continue jumping until its next turn.

A king moves one square diagonally in any direction, forwards or backwards, and captures by jumping in any direction, with further jumps in the same turn where possible. A king can be captured like any other piece.

Winning and drawing

You win when your opponent cannot move: either all their pieces have been captured, or those that remain are blocked. The game is a draw if the players agree that neither can force a win, which happens often when each has only a king or two left.

The old "huffing" rule

In many homes, a player who fails to make a capture has the offending piece removed ("huffed") by the opponent. This rule was dropped from the official game long ago. The modern rule is simply that an illegal move must be taken back and the capture made. Agree which way you are playing before you start.

Arguments this page settles

  • Must I take? Yes, always.
  • Can a plain man jump backwards? No, in English draughts.
  • Can a king fly the whole diagonal? No. In this game a king moves one square at a time. Long-range kings belong to the international game.
  • My man was crowned in the middle of a double jump. Can it carry on? No. The move ends when it is crowned.
  • Must I take the biggest capture? No, any capture will do.

Other kinds of draughts

GameBoardMain differences
English draughts (this page)8 by 8, 12 men eachMen move and capture forwards only; kings move one square
International draughts10 by 10, 20 men eachMen may capture backwards; kings move any distance along a diagonal ("flying kings"); the capture that takes the most pieces is compulsory
Russian and Brazilian draughts8 by 8, 12 men eachMuch like the international game on the small board, with flying kings and backward captures by men

If a visitor plays "wrongly", they may simply have learnt another country's game. Decide the rules together before the first move.

Questions people ask

Is draughts the same as checkers?

Yes. Draughts is the British name and checkers the American name for the same game on the 8 by 8 board.

Who goes first in draughts?

The player with the darker pieces. Players usually swap colours after each game.

Can you jump your own pieces?

No. You can only jump an enemy piece, and only when the square beyond it is empty.

Can a single man capture a king?

Yes. A king is captured in the same way as a man, by being jumped.
